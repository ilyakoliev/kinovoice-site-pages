Lermontov: A Journey Through Time to What Does Not Change with Time

In short

“Lermontov” is a 2025 film directed by Bakur Bakuradze. From the first frames the picture envelops you in the texture of the era and the place, literally letting you touch a way of life and of thinking.

BIBetsy Isakova·5 October 2025·updated 16 October 2025

About the film

Title
Lermontov · Лермонтов
Director
Bakur Bakuradze
Year
2025

5 October 2025

And in general Lermontov is an astonishing journey through time to what does not change with time.

From the first frames the picture envelops you in the texture of the era and the place, literally letting you touch a way of life and of thinking. We slow down together with the static camera, which does not hurry after the characters’ movements but records the overall picture almost impassively. Like fate, which is often woven from fatal mistakes.

In the poet’s last day before the duel there is nothing that would betray the singularity of the moment. On the contrary, this day seems to interest everyone except the protagonist. And Ilya Ozolin excellently conveys an ironic indifference to himself and the world, laconically and caustically joking against the backdrop of a village pastoral.

This film can be called a kind of antipode of Prorok (“The Prophet”, the Pushkin musical biopic). All they have in common is turning to the biographies of poetic geniuses. In everything else Lermontov is quieter, more modest, more realistic and more everyday, especially as regards the terrible moment of a senseless death. But death is not the end of the reflection, and Bakuradze places his full stop far more elegantly. Perhaps that is exactly why the film stands outside the competition: it is hard to judge the eternal truth about the senselessness of violence.

Catch it in cinemas from @volgafilms and @k24films from 16 October.

16 October 2025

From today Bakur Bakuradze’s beautiful Lermontov is in cinemas. Following my hot recommendation from Mayak (the film festival in Gelendzhik), I also want to highlight one significant detail for everyone who has yet to get acquainted with the picture.

I mean the epilogue scene. It is a very inventively created version, by the director, of an alternative universe in which everything could have turned out differently. Pay attention to the last scene of the film, where Lermontov, in his usual manner, sets up a prank on the husband of a lady who has been playing billiards with him. Everything is arranged so that (given a certain temperament in the husband) there would be every right to challenge the poet to a duel. But that does not happen. In this case what wins is the trust between the spouses, a sense of self-worth, the humanist principles of the value of life. In a word, everything that saves not only poets but people in general.

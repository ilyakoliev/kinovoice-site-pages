House of the Dragon: Seasons Two and Three, Episode by Episode

In short

16 July 2024

As gripping and tense (in a good way) as the fourth episode of House of the Dragon was, the fifth came out every bit as incoherent and empty, wrecking all the narrative momentum the season had been building until then.

The only thing I enjoyed watching was Daemon slowly losing his mind while one-eyed Aemond takes over his spot as “the wild one who’s fun to follow”. Except that in episode five even he was boring to follow. To say nothing of Alicent’s attempts to get through to that sulker Cole, or to the council in its all-male entirety (Rhaenyra has the same problem, but at least she has Mysaria now).

24 July 2024

A couple of words about episode 6 of House of the Dragon.

The stale rhythm of the last episode has been livened up a little, but still not enough for a situation called “the season ends in 2 episodes”. Sad as it is to admit, the storyline of Daemon’s chats with his own conscience has become, for me, that very spot in the series where you feel the urge to speed up playback or skip ahead. I expect this synchronised suffering of hero and audience to be rewarded with a triply impressive payoff (and in the meantime I’m consoling myself with memes).

Alicent, as was to be expected, has reaped exactly what she sowed. But her dismissal was far less epic than the business with Larys Strong: he and Aemond delivered the best exchange of the whole episode, if not the season. I thoroughly enjoyed watching this high-speed about-face in mid-air, at which Aemond proved even slicker than Larys himself, and that takes some doing. All that’s left is to look forward to the revenge of the humiliated and insulted Larys.

Rhaenyra’s entire role this season, meanwhile, seems to be mirrored in the scene of a knight’s (failed) attempt to tame a dragon. Rhaenyra stands off to one side and watches, mostly in silence. But the slap she deals a bore of a courtier and the “gifts” sent to King’s Landing bring back the days of the Rhaenyra who could mouth off to noble ladies and break up Daemon’s stand-off with Otto Hightower by flying in on a dragon. I hope that’s the heroine we’ll be watching for the rest of the season.

P.S. Before I had even watched episode 6, I learned from a post by colleagues that it would contain a certain scene Russian streaming services would have to cut. Deep down I was hoping it would finally be Daemon with Daemon in Daemon’s hallucinations :) But I spent the whole episode eyeing every pair of characters with suspicion. Lady Jeyne Arryn and Rhaena? Okay, I’m ready. Daemon and Viserys? Go on then, they’re hallucinations, we’ve seen all sorts in there already. But the show’s makers outdid my imagination: I didn’t see it coming, respect.

30 July 2024

Episode seven of House of the Dragon is, at last, lively, eventful and spectacular (we are heading for the season finale, after all).

The episode’s chief virtue, of course, is its wealth of meme-ready moments. From the stream of Targaryens of varying degrees of purity arriving at the Blacks’ castle (the shot got a decent round of jokes in the wake of Durov’s post about his sperm donation) to Daemon suddenly seeing a goat in the castle corridors (obviously both as his own reflection and as a reminder of Blackwood, the scapegoat he executed relatively undeservedly). And life, of course, teaches Aemond nothing, so he has once again run up against the fact that Vhagar cannot be controlled. I hope his careless driving won’t kill any more of Rhaenyra’s children.

A feature that is staking a claim to regular status this season, which I can only welcome: “The Most Epic Mid-Jump About-Face”. This time we’re admiring young Lord Tully, who didn’t just outplay Daemon but literally destroyed him morally, because the self-proclaimed king consort could never have imagined such a thing.

And the sulker of episode seven (no, Criston Cole took no part in the action), Alicent, simply walked off into the woods, so I’m already bracing myself for hallucinations like Daemon’s in the castle. Still, this shift in the balance between the two heroines towards the end of the season looks very harmonious: Rhaenyra began this season feeble (and has gained in resolve, not without Mysaria’s help); Alicent will (very probably) end it every bit as broken.

By the way, what was that moment with Mysaria in the last episode for, if in this one everything carries on as though nothing had happened? Of course, I don’t worry about their relationship any more than I do about my own, but I’d like to see it continued :)

31 July 2024

That said, it would be criminal to pass over how gorgeously Alicent’s flight into the woods is shot: simply an advert for a new Dior fragrance, nothing less.

6 August 2024

The finale of season two of House of the Dragon!

Experience had taught me to go in with fairly lowered expectations. Perhaps that was for the best, because the episode left me with a very pleasant impression: it is still pieces being moved into position, but with painstakingly worked-out surprises, intrigues and set-ups for new storylines. Something of a breath of fresh air after such a stuffy-ish season.

What freshened this episode up, of course, was every scene in Essos. Watching the Lannister and Admiral Lohar fight in a puddle of mud, I had a very pleasant feeling of being back in the Game of Thrones universe (it sounds very strange, I know, but that is where my associations went). We were shown two worlds crossing paths, fresh faces and customs, and the comic surprise of the admiral being a woman (and one with quite the tastes, too), and she was the one who caught our Lannister (who had come to hash things out with serious men) off guard.

Remember how I hoped that our long-suffering vigil over Daemon’s hallucinations would pay off with something super epic? Well, it did! More than unexpectedly, Daemon was granted a brief recap of 8 seasons of Game of Thrones, attained enlightenment and grasped his place in the universe. The insert was so sudden, so unlike the rest of the series even in its visual style, that at first I couldn’t believe my eyes. Nevertheless, HotD has taken a big step towards Winter, war and the other horrors that the descendants of these characters will face.

The suffering caused by Daemon’s storyline is over, and the suffering caused by Rhaena’s storyline has begun :) Her handful of scenes in this episode bored me more than Daemon’s entire storyline did all season. But such, it seems, is the road to a dragon (or a mountain wyrm, if some breakdowns are to be believed): I can only hope that Rhaenyra’s children stay in one piece and that the dragon finally ends up in the right hands (and that we never see this running around the hills again).

Finally, the regular feature for the episode’s outstanding dialogue goes to Rhaenyra and Alicent, who has come to her senses after her mini-retreat. Rhaenyra, with the authority of someone who has already tried to negotiate and has compromised her own moral principles more than once, as good as declares: “you’re no sister of mine any more”. Both as a viewer and in terms of the drama, I get it; too high a price has already been paid for Alicent’s intransigence: Rhaenys killed, Jace mortally offended, the lives of the false Targaryen bastards sacrificed (and riff-raff like Ulf let into the castle). It reminded me of one of the episodes of our episode-by-episode marathon V predydushchikh seriyakh (“Previously On”) on the first season of HotD. The hosts there reckoned that Rhaenyra and Alicent would surely have come to terms. Now we have been shown that, sadly, they twice failed to. And failing to come to terms is an outcome too, and a resolution for a season.

11 August 2024

What’s the secret of the House of the Dragon opening titles? (a post with sound)

These titles are a bonus in their own right to the plot of season 2. In them the whole history of House Targaryen is embroidered, as it were, on a tapestry (which is also a nod to the fact that Helaena, with her gift of foresight, is often shown to us at her needlework, and her embroideries surely foretell something too).

By the way, compare the titles of the first episode with those of the last and you’ll see which plot details have been added (from the execution of the ratcatchers to the funeral procession with the beheaded infant). And here is what’s going on in the screenshots with the Targaryens’ history, in the order of my screenshots:

  • The Doom of Valyria: a natural cataclysm that wiped out a great many dragons and every dragon-owning family except the Targaryens. Hence their chosen status and unique power over dragons by the time HotD begins. In the embroidery you can make out the silhouettes of a volcanic eruption and of falling dragons.

  • Aegon the Conqueror crushes the Lannisters and the Gardeners (yes, he crushed the latter so thoroughly that we never even heard their name in either series): look closely at the shields of the fallen warriors and you can see the Lion and the Hand, elements of the coats of arms of the subjugated houses.

  • The death of Maegor the Cruel: nobody knows for certain whether the Iron Throne killed this Targaryen or he chose to take his own life, but as a picture of what power does to a person it is telling

  • The fourth image is familiar to us from the first season: the very council at which Viserys, not Rhaenys, was chosen as heir to the throne (and how calm and nice everything could have been)

3 July 2026

Two episodes in, the new season of House of the Dragon looks packed.

And although its opening scenes give no such impression at all, it quickly makes amends. That familiar feeling of “hang on just a little longer, it’s about to kick off”, which we had more than our fill of over the first two seasons, quickly gives way to “whoa, this as well?”. Ideally, of course, the rhythm of the action would be evened out somehow, but maybe it really is a strategy.

Don’t get me wrong, but at the start of the season the secondary characters’ storylines were a bit of a nuisance. Even for the second season they looked like fairly recent arrivals, and at the start of the third they leave you mildly baffled: is this really what we’ve all gathered for? Then again, let me try once more to rationalise this on the showrunners’ behalf: if it’s a plan to lull us into a false sense of security before a run of sharp twists, it worked a treat (apart from the fact that for the first half of the opening episode I was almost physically fighting the urge to stop watching).

11 July 2026

As for the original House of the Dragon, so far it is a pleasure, albeit with some very unoriginal ideas.

To put the phrase “heavy is the cap of Monomakh” (the Russian equivalent of “heavy is the head that wears the crown”) on screen, the series has slowed its pace considerably, all but locking the episode’s action within the four walls of the royal castle. It turns out that the long-awaited “after” of Rhaenyra taking the throne instantly turns into “all that talk, and for this?”.

I was amused by the neatly condensed chain of searches for the state treasury, or at least for the nobleman in charge of it. Anyone who has had the good fortune to work somewhere even remotely corporate knows the situation: the project is old, more than one team has come and gone on it, and then comes the moment when the successors discover to their horror that there is no documentation and no staff who hold the information either.

Same with Rhaenyra: she got no onboarding and no welcome pack, nothing but worries and rats.

23 July 2026

A few notes as I watch House of the Dragon

  • Sulkers don’t change. Especially if they’re knights. Especially if it’s Criston Cole. As a result, the storylines that form around him are predictable (because of the stupidity of what goes on), and their outcome comes as a surprise only to the characters whose lot it is to witness it all, not to the viewer.

  • Psychological rebirths do Targaryens good. Especially one-eyed ones. Especially when there’s a witch with a powerful moral compass close by. One of the most interesting storylines right now is unfolding around Aemond Targaryen. He is also at the centre of a heap of conspiracy theories (right up to the idea that he could have become the leader of the White Walkers who appeared to Daemon in a vision at the end of the second season). None of these theories is very convincing, but they are telling as a symptom of the audience’s interest in the character.

  • Had the history of the Targaryens gone a little differently, perhaps their totem animal would have been cats rather than dragons. Either way, thank you, episode five, for that sweet detail.

  • New characters who arrive with a rich context and a properly scripted personality from the off are great. Ormund Hightower is the prime proof of that (unlike certain characters whose only reason for taking up screen time is an inferiority complex about not having a dragon). On the whole, by the middle of the third season you start to feel something forgotten since Game of Thrones, the sense of a three-dimensional, densely populated world, and that is a pleasure.

10 August 2026

And actually, the season 3 finale of House of the Dragon is greatness.

If we’re dealing in comparisons with Game of Thrones, it is at least on the same level, if not better. We were shown everything:

  • a magnificent battle scene (with the horrors of war in detail)
  • attempts at wise diplomacy (which, as in life, nobody appreciated)
  • the despair of a queen as the only real hostage of power
  • comedy on the edge of madness (does the cry “I’m alive, imbecile” ring any bells?)

The way this episode spans genres is truly admirable. The writers have laid on a whole array of emotional rides, brilliant performances from at least 3 actors (in my subjective opinion, the ones who shone here were the actors playing Rhaenyra, Gwayne and Aegon) and an extremely brisk pace (those who watched the season know that far from every episode can be praised for that). In short, watching the season for this episode alone is more than worth it; as a resolution before a two-year break it is wonderful in every sense.

House of the Dragon: Seasons Two and Three, Episode by Episode

In short

“House of the Dragon” is a 2022 series directed by Clare Kilner, Geeta Vasant Patel. It is still a positioning of forces, but with painstakingly thought-out surprises, intrigues and set-ups for new storylines.

BIBetsy Isakova·16 July 2024·updated 10 August 2026

About the series

Title
House of the Dragon
Director
Clare Kilner, Geeta Vasant Patel
Year
2022

16 July 2024

As gripping and good-tensely the fourth episode of House of the Dragon was, the fifth turned out just as incoherent and empty, collapsing the whole rhythm of the narrative, which had been gathering pace over the season.

The only thing I enjoyed watching was Daemon slowly going mad, while his place as “the reckless guy who is interesting to follow” is taken by one-eyed Aemond. Only in the fifth episode even he was boring to follow. Let alone Alicent’s attempts to make contact with sulky Cole or with the council in its full male line-up (Rhaenyra has the same problem, but at least she has Mysaria now).

24 July 2024

A couple of words about episode 6 of House of the Dragon.

The stale rhythm of the previous episode is livened up a little, but still not enough for a situation called “the season ends in 2 episodes”. Sad as it is to admit, the storyline of Daemon’s conversations with his own conscience has become for me that very place in the series where I want to speed up or skip. I expect that these synchronous torments of the hero and the viewers must be justified by a triply impressive resolution (and meanwhile I console myself with memes).

Alicent, as was to be expected, got what she fought for. But her dismissal was far less epic than the situation with Larys Strong: he and Aemond delivered the best dialogue of the whole episode, if not the season. I watched with great pleasure this high-speed mid-air change of shoes, in which Aemond outdid Larys himself, and that takes some doing. It remains to look forward to what the revenge of the humiliated and insulted Larys will be like.

But Rhaenyra’s whole role in this season seems to be reflected in the scene of a knight’s (failed) attempt to tame a dragon. Rhaenyra stands to one side and watches, mostly in silence. But the slap delivered to a tiresome courtier and the “gifts” sent to King’s Landing bring back the days of the Rhaenyra who could be rude to noble ladies and break up Daemon’s clash with Otto Hightower by flying in on a dragon. I hope that for the rest of the season we will be watching exactly that heroine.

P.S. Even before watching episode 6 I learned from a colleagues’ post that it would have a certain scene that our domestic streaming services would have to cut. Deep down I hoped it would finally be Daemon with Daemon in Daemon’s hallucinations) But the whole episode I eyed every pair of characters with suspicion. Lady Jeyne Arryn and Rhaena? Okay, I am ready. Daemon and Viserys? Go on, they are hallucinations, we have seen all sorts there already. But the creators outdid my imagination; it was unexpected, I respect it

30 July 2024

The seventh episode of House of the Dragon is at last brisk, eventful and spectacular (things are heading for the season finale, after all).

The episode’s main merit, of course, is the abundance of meme-worthy moments. Starting with the stream of Targaryens of varying degrees of purity arriving at the Blacks’ castle (this shot was nicely joked about in the wake of Durov’s post about his sperm donation) and ending with Daemon suddenly seeing a goat in the castle corridors (obviously both as his own reflection and as a reminder of the scapegoat Blackwood, executed by him relatively undeservedly). And Aemond, of course, has learned nothing from life, so he once again ran into Vhagar’s unmanageability. I hope he will not kill any more of Rhaenyra’s children with such careless driving.

A segment that is claiming permanent status this season, which cannot but please: “The most epic mid-jump change of shoes”. This time we admire the young Lord Tully, who did not merely outplay Daemon but literally destroyed him morally, because the self-proclaimed king consort could not have imagined such a thing.

And the sulker of the seventh episode (no, Criston Cole did not take part in the action), Alicent, simply went off into the forest, so I am already mentally preparing for hallucinations like Daemon’s in the castle. However, such a shift of balance between the two heroines towards the end of the season looks very harmonious: as feeble as Rhaenyra began this season (gaining resolve not without Mysaria’s help), so shattered will Alicent (very probably) end it.

By the way, what was the moment with Mysaria in the previous episode about, if in this one everything is as if nothing had happened? I worry about their relationship no more than about my own, of course, but I would like to see a continuation)

6 August 2024

The finale of season two of House of the Dragon!

From experience I approached the viewing with fairly lowered expectations. Perhaps that was for the best, because I came away from the episode with a very pleasant impression: it is still a positioning of forces, but with painstakingly thought-out surprises, intrigues and set-ups for new storylines. A breath of fresh air after such a stuffy season.

Of course, what freshened up this episode were all the scenes in Essos. Watching the fight between a Lannister and the admiral Lohar in a mud puddle, I had a very pleasant feeling of returning to the universe of Game of Thrones (it sounds very strange, I understand, but such are the associations). They showed the intersection of two worlds, fresh faces, mores, the comic surprise of the admiral being a woman (and one of certain tastes at that), and it was she who surprised our Lannister (who had come to hash things out with serious men).

Remember how I hoped that our long-suffering watching of Daemon’s hallucinations would be justified by something super epic? And so it happened! Quite unexpectedly Daemon was granted a brief recap of 8 seasons of Game of Thrones, was filled with it and realised his place in the universe. This insert was so sudden, so different even in visual style from the rest of the series, that at first I did not believe my eyes. Nevertheless, HotD took a big step towards Winter, war and the other horrors the descendants of the protagonists will face.

The suffering from Daemon’s storyline ended, and the suffering from Rhaena’s storyline began) Over a few scenes with her in this episode I was more bored than over the whole of Daemon’s line in the season. But such, evidently, is the path to a dragon (or a mountain wyrm, if you believe some analyses): I can only hope that Rhaenyra’s children stay in one piece too and that the dragon finally ends up in the right hands (and that we will not see any more of this running about the hills).

Finally, into the permanent segment of the episode’s outstanding dialogue go Rhaenyra and Alicent, who has come to her senses after her mini-retreat. Rhaenyra, as someone who has already tried to come to terms and has more than once compromised her own moral principles, effectively declares: “you are no longer a sister to me”. Both as a viewer and dramatically I understand this; too high a price has already been paid for Alicent’s intransigence: Rhaenys killed, Jace mortally offended, the lives of false Targaryen bastards sacrificed (and such marginal figures as Ulf admitted to the castle). I remembered one of the episodes of our episode-by-episode marathon V predydushchikh seriyakh (“Previously On”) about the first season of HotD. The hosts there assumed that Rhaenyra and Alicent would certainly come to an agreement. Now we have been shown that, alas, they twice failed to agree. And failing to agree is also a result and a resolution for a season.

3 July 2026

Judging by its first two episodes, the new season of House of the Dragon looks eventful.

And although from the first scenes it makes no such impression at all, it quickly corrects itself. That very feeling of “wait just a little longer and it’s about to start”, of which we had our fill over the first two seasons, quickly gives way to “whoa, that too?”. If possible, of course, it would be good to balance the rhythm of the action somehow, but maybe that is actually a strategy.

At the start of the season, do not misunderstand me, the storylines of the secondary characters were a little tiresome. Even in the second season they looked rather recently introduced, and at the start of the third they provoke mild bewilderment: is this what we gathered for? Although, once again I will try to rationalise it for the showrunners: if it is a plan to lull our vigilance before a succession of cool twists, it worked perfectly (not counting that for the first half of the opening episode I almost physically fought the urge to stop watching).

11 July 2026

As for the original House of the Dragon, it is pleasing so far, albeit with rather unoriginal ideas

To film the phrase “heavy is the cap of Monomakh” (the Russian equivalent of “heavy is the head that wears the crown”), the series slowed its pace considerably, practically locking the episode’s action within the four walls of the royal castle. It turns out that the long-awaited “after” of Rhaenyra taking the throne instantly turns into “so much for all that talk”.

I was amused by the concisely shown chain of searches for the state treasury, or at least for the nobleman responsible for it. Anyone who has had the good fortune to work in an even slightly corporate environment knows this situation: the project is old, more than one team has changed while working on it, and then comes the moment when the successors discover in horror that there is neither documentation nor any staff who possess the information.

So it was with Rhaenyra: she got neither onboarding nor a welcome pack, only worries and rats.

23 July 2026

A few remarks along the way while watching House of the Dragon

  • Sulkers do not change. Especially if they are knights. Especially if it is Criston Cole. Thus predictable storylines (because of the stupidity of what happens) form around him, whose outcome is a surprise only to the characters who happen to witness it all, not to the viewer.

  • Psychological rebirths do the Targaryens good. Especially the one-eyed ones. Especially if there is a witch with a powerful moral compass nearby. One of the most interesting storylines is now developing around Aemond Targaryen. Around him, too, there is a heap of conspiracy theories (up to his possibly becoming the leader of the White Walkers who appeared to Daemon in his vision at the end of the second season). All these theories are unconvincing, but they are telling as a symptom of the audience’s interest in the character.

  • If the history of the Targaryens had developed a little differently, perhaps their totem animal would have become cats instead of dragons. In any case, thank you to the fifth episode for this sweet detail.

  • New characters who are introduced straight away with a voluminous context and a personality worked out in the script are superb. Ormund Hightower is the main proof of this (unlike some characters whose only reason for taking up screen time is an inferiority complex in the absence of a dragon). On the whole, by the middle of the third season a feeling forgotten since Game of Thrones began to be felt while watching, that of a three-dimensional, densely populated world, and it is pleasant.

10 August 2026

And in general, the finale of season 3 of House of the Dragon is greatness.

If we operate with comparisons to Game of Thrones, it is at least the same level, if not superior. We were shown everything:

  • a magnificent battle scene (with the detailed horrors of war)
  • attempts at wise diplomacy (which, as in life, nobody appreciated)
  • the despair of a queen as the only real hostage of power
  • a comedy on the edge of madness (does the cry “I’m alive, imbecile” ring any bells?)

The multi-genre nature of this episode provokes real admiration. The screenwriters provided a multitude of emotional attractions, brilliant performances from at least 3 actors (in my subjective opinion, the performers of Rhaenyra, Gwayne and Aegon distinguished themselves here) and an extremely brisk pace (viewers of the season know that not every episode deserves praise for this). In a word, watching the season for this episode alone is more than worth it; the resolution before a two-year pause is wonderful in every sense.

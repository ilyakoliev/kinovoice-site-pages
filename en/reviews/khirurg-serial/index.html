The Surgeon: A Finale Broken Down into Pluses and Minuses

In short

“The Surgeon” is a 2025 series directed by Ilya Ermolov. The first half of the series is noticeably stronger than the second, in plot, in dialogue and in dynamics.

BIBetsy Isakova·4 August 2025·updated 30 August 2025

About the series

Title
The Surgeon · Хирург
Director
Ilya Ermolov
Year
2025

4 August 2025

And in general The Surgeon (Khirurg) is a wonderful story about a rare type of hero who can be described as “a bad good man”.

A transplant surgeon who wanted to distance himself from the terrible reality of how organs are obtained ended up a victim of his own indifference. He is an excellent doctor and father, and means well, but a bad husband and a cruel man, and that is his problem.

Sergei Gilev looks perfectly contradictory in this role: combining toughness with sincerity, he creates a hero who evokes empathy but not pity. Though I am probably on his side anyway, because the other characters (except the daughter) are mostly just bad people.

I hope this story steers towards a finale where bad good people also deserve relative happiness.

8 August 2025

The fifth episode of The Surgeon works as a great development of the long set-up we were given in the first four episodes. The method of narration changes too: no more flashbacks, if you look closely.

It is pleasing that Yulia, Marina Vorozhishcheva’s heroine, comes to the fore: it is not often you see a promotion from “damsel in distress” to a fully fledged protagonist; now she is a subject, not an object (if only briefly, yes). This is especially important at the moment when the first loop of the surgeon Sergei’s storyline seems to be complete: what the hero would do next without Yulia is not very clear. Gilev, by the way, is still magnificent. Three episodes remain to sort out all the problems and enjoy the acting (and it really is a joy to the eyes and the soul)

30 August 2025

The finale of The Surgeon puzzled me, so I will break it down into pluses and minuses:

👆A happy ending for Sergei, his daughter and her suitor. Vera seems to have ended up satisfied too (this heroine’s soul is probably a mystery)

👆Unambiguously closed storylines for a multitude of characters (which, for such a number of them, is rather a rarity)

👆Genuinely convincing (for the most part) performances

👇Overly schematic endings; in such cases I always argue that adding an episode would have been a good idea (for example, to reveal Batyr’s plan and whether or not Sergei was involved in it)

👇 So Vera is a rat twice over? Men would do better to be on their guard with her, otherwise there is a high risk of going to prison for their misdeeds. In general, a slight demonisation of the female character took place.

👇The first half of the series is noticeably stronger than the second, in plot, in dialogue and in dynamics. A pity that such a drop occurred; the story had very great potential indeed.

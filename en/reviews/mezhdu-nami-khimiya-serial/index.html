Chemo Romance: A Finale of Mixed Feelings and an Honest Look at Illness

In short

“Chemo Romance” is a 2025 series directed by Karina Chuvikova. The series shows the situation with the illness honestly and without speculation.

BIBetsy Isakova·9 March 2025·updated 18 April 2025

About the series

Title
Chemo Romance · Между нами химия
Director
Karina Chuvikova
Year
2025

9 March 2025

As it happens, I started the series Chemo Romance (Mezhdu nami khimiya, “There’s Chemistry Between Us”) while rewatching (and finishing) Breaking Bad in parallel. The result was a parallel between the male and female views of one and the same plot set-up.

The heroine of Chemo Romance, Tanya, works as a taxi driver, is raising two children alone (her husband died), and suddenly learns that she has cancer. Now her main task is to earn money for the rest of her life to find a husband to whom she can leave the children, otherwise they will go to an orphanage.

And if Breaking Bad is largely a reflection of the male midlife crisis (only, instead of realising that death is near, the hero literally gets a near death from cancer), Chemo Romance is, as they say, just another Tuesday for a woman. Over the first two episodes Sasha Bortich played every shade of despair: at her diagnosis, at a friend’s betrayal, at not quite measuring up to men’s high demands, and at having to tell her children the terrible truth. It seems the whole world has let her down, but there is the ghostly beginning of a new storyline with Nikita Efremov’s character, and that is the best reason to wait for more)

It is great that a series about woman power like this came out for 8 March (International Women’s Day): sincere, ironic, touching to tears, but instilling an important thought: you should think not about death but about life.

26 March 2025

I continue watching Chemo Romance and observe two things.

1️⃣ How differently (and that is great!) Tanya from Chemo Romance and Natasha Kustova from Pochka (“The Kidney”) fight for their goals. Bortich’s heroine has a truly soft strength, a little naive, vulnerable, held up, you could say, by the viewers’ hopes. Now we rejoice not simply in strong female characters but in their variety (hello to Outsource here too)

2️⃣ How great that Nikita Efremov too got to play such a double role (following the year’s film trend after Mickey 17, Amura, Kalimba, Vasily and other projects with twins played by one actor). It only remains to understand whether it is good that, of the two plot options, in which he could have turned out to be a ghost or a living twin of the dead man, they chose the second)

3 April 2025

Chemo Romance, what are you doing?!

As if Alexandra Bortich’s heroine’s relationship with her children did not already make my eyes well up every episode, they have also decided to move us with music. Specifically, the song “Ogni goroda” (“City Lights”) performed by Kuzma Kotrelyov and the band Eshchyo Nikak. Maximum respect to the guys for the song (to Kuzma specifically for writing the lyrics), and to the series’ composer Dasha Charusha for the find: it is a stunning scene and an endless repeat for the next couple of days.

Sorry, but in the rivalry between Efremov’s and Kotrelyov’s characters a favourite has sharply begun to emerge.

18 April 2025

Chemo Romance is over. The finale left me with mixed feelings (and tears), but first things first)

It is great that Tanya’s choice cannot be predicted to the very last, precisely because the heroine is three-dimensional, and it is not obvious whether she will choose personal, if short, happiness or the long-term relative well-being of her children. The stars of the script aligned so that these incompatible things coincided, but before that there were so many chances to take the wrong turn that the happy resolution turned out to be rather a miracle.

The relatively happy ending looks a little smudged; perhaps the possibility of a second season left open makes it so (I had similar emotions from the finale of the first season of Pochka, which was indeed continued). By the way, if you compare with Pochka, Tanya turned out to be no less a manipulator than Natasha Kustova, the only difference being a sense of guilt towards the people deceived) But Efremov’s character, as it turned out, was “glad to be deceived himself” (as Pushkin put it) In my view, Tanya had a great and sincere feeling only with Kotrelev’s character, but it turned out not to be meant to be.

For all the debatable moments of the melodramatic structure of Chemo Romance, it has an indisputable advantage. The series shows the situation with the illness honestly and without speculation. The disease is serious, but nobody was planning to keel over and die straight away, because there is therapy. This is a great realistic look at the situation, which is not found everywhere. In Aktyorishche (“A Hell of an Actor”), for instance (and this is very disappointing), when the protagonist is found to have multiple sclerosis, everyone simply folds their hands and grieves that in a year he will be in a wheelchair, while the doctors shake their heads, admitting the situation is hopeless. In reality, even for such diseases there is effective treatment. It would be good if all series touching on such serious matters treated the subject as conscientiously as Chemo Romance)

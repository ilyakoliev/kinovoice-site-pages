Chef: Principled Kai, Who Has an Ember Instead of a Heart

19 November 2024

Well, I really did watch 6 episodes of Chef (Shef) in a row (yes, the cunning @fridaytv, the Pyatnitsa channel, held back the 7th and 8th all the way until Sunday; they did promise to bring it to the boil!) And it really was good.

Our initial expectations were justified thanks to the story of principled Kai (who, contrary to his name, has not a splinter of ice instead of a heart, as in The Snow Queen, but an ember). Off the top of your head you cannot name other contemporary heroes who could utter the words “I’m not going anywhere, because this is my home, and anyway, why should I?” (episode 2). Denis Prytkov delivered his second stunning role of the year (the first was Ona takaya klassnaya, “She’s So Cool”), and it is simply a celebration.

As the story developed, the focus spread to other characters, or rather heroines: the women working in the prison colony’s kitchen. There is nothing complicated in this part; the mechanism of a team getting used to a new manager (our Kai) is a traditional part of any film about the path to a dream, overcoming everything on the way to the goal and success. But the main advantage of this part lies in its proportion and transparency. The prisoners do not reform in 5 minutes, dazzled by the greatness of the chef who has fallen on their heads, but keep showing their character until the respect of each of them is won. And despite the modest running time, Kai’s achieving mutual respect with each of the heroines is shown, spoken and dramatically justified (and not, may the series Kombinatsiya forgive me, played out as a silent scene to cheerful music instead of a convincing speech by the hero against that background). Things are fine with the music too, where it is present and needed: Dayte Tank (!) plays, and that I respect.

Towards the finale Pyatnitsa pulls a trump card from its sleeve and turns episode 7 into what it does best. Yes, yes, into a reality show. Well, with such material it would be a sin not to wrap it in its favourite packaging. And that is good too. We have got to know Kai and his new team from the prison kitchen well enough. Now we can step back and look at it all in the third person. And from what angle this series will serve us its dessert episode, we will see on Sunday.

26 November 2024

I thought for a long time about how to understand the finale of the series Chef. Then I realised it reminds me of this photograph: beautiful and at the same time chaotic, but worth looking at anyway.

On the one hand, in the finale, as in the whole series, everything fits screw to screw, the drama works and passions run high. The kind of content I do not speed up, because the dynamics are wonderful as they are) Prytkov is superb, the other characters’ images have fully opened up and come alive by the finale, and all the scenes with the opening of the restaurant are executed with filigree precision.

On the other hand, for all my love of the series, I began to notice its craftedness: the little seams needed to move the action, coincidences in the characters’ fates that try to pass for cinematic conventions. It seems too conventional, both the twist with the medical record forgotten in his own pocket and, even more so, Kai being jailed for a year. Good in script terms, but is Kai not too clever a hero for that?

While I was thinking, I read an interview by colleagues at Kinopoisk with Denis Prytkov (that is where the photo comes from). I found no minuses; it is definitely the best companion to the series, and as proof, here is a quote (about food and about love, of course):

— Do you have a signature dish?

— French-style meat! That’s how I won my wife’s heart. Once, before Covid, I made her invite me over. I spent my last money on groceries and went to cook at hers. Sadly, I didn’t stay the night, but the dish, as history has shown, worked.

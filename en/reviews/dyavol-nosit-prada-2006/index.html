The Devil Wears Prada: Minus One Point for Andy for Lacking Journalistic Curiosity

In short

“The Devil Wears Prada” is a 2006 film directed by David Frankel. Surprisingly enough, the most interesting characters in the sequel for me will be Emily and Nigel.

BIBetsy Isakova·10 May 2026

About the film

Title
The Devil Wears Prada
Director
David Frankel
Year
2006

I spent yesterday evening rewatching The Devil Wears Prada, so today I am studying the brand’s website have some observations to share. There will definitely be spoilers below, so if for some reason you want to avoid them while reading about a 20-year-old film, be careful.

  • The fact that Andy (Anne Hathaway) came to apply for a job at a fashion glossy having prepared nothing but her brilliant CV with a note about editing the university newspaper is either a sign of a lost approach to hiring or the heroine’s glaring irresponsibility. If the former, then we, it turns out, have lost the chance to get by on professional skills alone, without also elbowing our way to expertise in the subject of a particular medium. If the latter, then Andy simply approached the matter unprofessionally. Just as any self-respecting journalist prepares for an interview with a particular subject, building the conversation around that person, so, when coming to work at a fashion glossy, it would have been natural to brush up on the basics in order to learn the surname Gabbana. Here Andy gets minus one point from me for lacking basic journalistic curiosity.

  • I also felt like going over Andy’s personal life from the point of view of logic. If the attraction to the veteran journalist has already become mutual, why run away from him, slamming the door (and, let us admit, losing a certain circle of career privileges)? If she wants to return to the relationship with her original boyfriend while getting rid of the irregular hours, why take a job in New York rather than go with him to Boston? The film handles the romantic aspect far more schematically than the career one: the heroine’s companions turn literally into symbols either of career temptations and intrigues (Christian the journalist) or of simplicity and naivety outside the glossy world (Nate the boyfriend). What will happen to the heroine’s personal life in the second part with such schematisation is frightening to think about.

  • Surprisingly enough, the most interesting characters in the sequel for me will be Emily and Nigel. They are contradictory, both to a certain extent villainously charismatic and, on the whole, deeper than either Andy or Miranda. In essence, it is they who personify the world of glossy magazines in the film, embodying its pluses and minuses.

  • No questions for Miranda Priestly. That is all.

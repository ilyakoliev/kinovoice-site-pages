Children of Change: Three Paths, Raves, Vodka and IT, Adjusted for 1995

In short

“Children of Change” is a 2024 series directed by Sergey Taramaev, Lyubov Lvova. “Russia”, as the song went, “has three paths: raves, vodka and IT”.

21 November 2024

“Russia”, as the song went, “has three paths: raves, vodka and IT”. That (adjusted for 1995) is what the series Children of Change (Deti peremen), which launched today with two episodes, is about.

What is fresh in the exposition: an unusual family of a mother and three sons by different fathers (our “three paths”, a great opening). What is familiar: the ubiquitous 1990s. Perhaps such a prominent series should have been released at some distance from Daring (Likhie), which also lays claim to being a family saga in the middle of hard times, but let us watch the competition!

I think the series will not get lost, because at the start we have:

  • Charismatic and distinctive representatives of opposite worlds. Viktoria Isakova as Flora, the mother of three lads, passionate about life no matter what. And Timofey Tribuntsev as Zhigalin, the unhinged, infernal leader of an organised crime group who does evil on an unearthly scale

  • Contrasting types of young people: a philosophising gangster (Slava Kopeikin, tormented by Hamletian questions), an anarchic artist (Makar Khlebnikov, so far my favourite of all the sons; he draws film posters, mind you) and the youngest son, who has not yet shown himself in any particular way (perhaps, in this context, thank God; he will stay in one piece longer), Khetag Khinchagov

  • The supporting cast: the wonderful Alexander Yatsenko, Artyom Bystrov and Lev Zulkarnaev (somewhere in the series, they say, he will recite poems of his own composition!)

  • An interesting dramatic engine in the mother’s ignorance of what her eldest son does. She will, of course, have to find out that he is a gangster, but what happens then is the intrigue. That is what we keep watching for.

30 January 2026

Children of Change 2 started straight off at a gallop

The first episode obviously implies a certain number of events left off screen; there is room here for flashbacks, which I hope we will see over the season. That would be a harmonious link between the “before” and “after” of all the characters, for whose understanding some context is needed, literally from Flora (Viktoria Isakova in heroin chic) to Genrikh (the endlessly many-faced Sergei Gilev).

What is striking, though, is how the new season immediately divides the characters into those who managed to reassemble themselves and survive in hard times, and those who stayed themselves and therefore did not make it into the future. Perhaps this will become the key theme of the season: to give up a part of yourself and live on, or to stay true to yourself but perish.

Comeback: The Air of the 2000s Wafts Through the Screen with Unfeigned Youth

In short

“Comeback” is a 2025 series directed by Dinar Garipov. It’s set in the 2000s, and the air of that era literally wafts through the screen with a kind of unfeigned youthfulness.

11 October 2025

I’ve finally got round to Comeback (Kambek), an ultra-atmospheric series with Alexander Petrov as a homeless man who has lost his memory.

It’s set in the 2000s, and the air of that era literally wafts through the screen with a kind of unfeigned youthfulness. That atmosphere is heightened by the teenage leads — strong, brave, wise in their own way. And it is all set to tracks by Mujuice, who always gets right to the depths of your soul. And the plot, of course, brings out something unhackneyed, episode after episode, about friendship and about kindness to your neighbour and to yourself. Simple and tasteful.

Watching it gave me feelings I had long forgotten, not felt since Peace! Friendship! Chewing Gum! (the 1990s-set series), so I’m with this series to the end.

18 October 2025

Still watching adoring Comeback

The fourth episode is a perfect example of an episode’s vertical plot complementing the story’s overall horizontal structure so organically that it’s hard even to tell them apart. My thanks to the show’s makers and to @russian_c0de for work this thorough, of course.

The series is at its halfway point and picking up speed very skilfully. The record viewing figures say as much, eloquently. That kind of demand, it seems to me, can be explained by interest in the chosen era. All of us, to one degree or another (younger viewers especially), come from the 2000s — from that culture, that starting point for how we see the world and ourselves. And revisiting your relative youth always draws you in.

20 October 2025

A separate pleasure is the aesthetic of the school that the kids in Comeback go to

For me these are the landscapes of home. What matters most is that it is an ordinary school (even if that only came about because of the period — in 2004 all schools were like that). I went to exactly this kind of school, and my eye delights in the familiar set-up of the classrooms, with those peeling wooden chairs (which were forever snagging your tights — the girls will know what I mean), the scuffed lino, the wooden shelving, the wide windowsills and the haphazard plants on them. The canteen was a little bigger than the one in the still from the series, but with the same serving line of metal pots with red lettering. In my last years there, admittedly, the chalkboards were replaced with whiteboards and then, in part, with electronic ones, but everything around them stayed just as it was :)

You hardly ever see schools like that on screen these days, and certainly not in stories about the present — everywhere there has to be a swimming pool, a canteen with a hotel-style buffet and ultra-trendy refurbishments. Schools like that really do exist, but old-style schools exist and operate alongside them (I visited mine last year — the authenticity is all still there). And it is a real pleasure to recognise on screen a little piece of my own youth, which, it turns out, can even serve as a film set.

31 October 2025

Episode six of Comeback very neatly takes many of the storylines off the table — precisely to put the emphasis on a new variable, Alexey Chadov’s mysterious character. He is obviously going to be one of the main players in the two final episodes, and that calls for a little extra room, both artistically and in running time.

A separate joy is the quotation of a scene from the film Brother (Balabanov’s 1997 cult classic). If you remember, at one point Danila tries to stand up for Sveta, the tram driver, against her husband, who beats her. Having put the man out of action, Danila asks her to leave with him, but she stays with her husband, who is suffering from a bullet in the leg and will obviously go on beating her. The same thing is repeated almost shot for shot in Comeback and, you might say, has now reached the level of an archetypal plot.

By the way, I’m watching the series very closely so that I can log every episode in a Kinopoisk piece — if you find yourself mulling over the details, drop by.

16 November 2025

And the crowning touch to the finale of Comeback is the news of a second season.

In the last episode the density of events went off the scale, but, happily, none of the storylines suffered for it. That said, I preferred the first half of the story, where, for one thing, the kids still seemed to have everything ahead of them (😭), and for another, there was enough time to take in each event. But the last episodes have a drive of their own too, combined with the sense of an era slipping away — an era of great adventure and the carefree early 2000s.

What will I be looking for in season 2? Updated versions of the kids (in particular, developments in Dasha’s love life, with her assorted candidates and with Petya, who vanished as suddenly as he appeared). A late-00s look that, in the show’s tradition, isn’t glossy, naturally. And Kompot’s life getting back to normal: showing the hardships of a homeless man’s life is half the job; it is far harder to portray honestly the difficulties of his fitting back into society and overcoming the stigma that trails behind him from his former status.

It’s great that the show has deservedly been renewed, and I’m genuinely looking forward to the new season.

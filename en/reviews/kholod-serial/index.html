Cold: A Series So Immersive It Knocks You Off Your Feet

In short

“Cold” is a 2026 series directed by Aleksey Kazakov. The series, and Lyubov Aksyonova’s performance in particular, has a kind of immersive pull that knocks you off your feet.

Betsy Isakova·16 July 2026·updated 22 September 2026

About the series

Title
Cold · Холод · also known as Kholod, Xolod
Director
Aleksey Kazakov
Year
2026

IMDb ↗ · Kinopoisk ↗

16 July 2026

From today you can watch Cold (Kholod), a bone-chilling series with Lyubov Aksyonova.

Watching the first episode, I literally lost track of time: from about the fifth minute I just sat with my head in my hands, trying not to start spelling out, aloud and in unprintable terms, the scale of the catastrophe in the plot. So many misfortunes befall Zhenya, Aksyonova’s character, that at times you genuinely just forget to breathe. It’s exactly the kind of series during which you’re never once tempted to check your phone.

And Zhenya herself is so resilient, hardy and patient that it’s hard to refuse her your sympathy, and the urge to root for her is the main motivation to watch to the end. Every scene shows very bluntly just what real hardships we’re talking about (emotional and physical), without sparing the viewer and without trying to, as it were, “hide” the story’s uncomfortable moments off screen. A series of rare sincerity about the powerlessness of the little person in the face of fate’s unjust blows.

6 August 2026

In a recent interview with Sobchak (the Russian TV journalist), Lyubov Aksyonova told a story that is hard to forget: her father spent 4 years in a penal colony and then proved his innocence in court. She was 19. Now she plays Zhenya in Cold, a drama from Ivi, START and Wink (Russian streaming services) about a woman unjustly convicted over a crash. Once you know that, you see the role in a completely different light.

Today my interview with Lyubov Aksyonova, Linda Lapiņš and Alexandra Babaskina came out in The Blueprint. We talked about trauma — how it breaks you, makes you grow up and sometimes becomes a source of strength — and about how to stay human through all these ordeals.

Inside there is plenty the trailer won’t tell you: why Aksyonova, for the first time in her career, drew up a detailed map of her character, and why the early Zhenya went by the code name “Snegurochka” (the Snow Maiden); Lapiņš’s true story of a girl whose fate seemed sealed — and ended at Yale University; and why Sasha Babaskina is sure that children are sometimes stronger than adults.

The piece has come out insanely beautiful — not only in its ideas but visually too: special love to the photographer Maxim Tarakanov and the whole team.

As always, I’ve picked the quote that is key for me — it comes from Lyubov Aksyonova ⤵️

Cold is a story about how, even out of the most terrible ordeal, a person always has the chance to come through to love and lean on it. Love can prove stronger than the desire to destroy. And only love can truly conquer death. And that, I suppose, is the most important thing for me.

Incidentally, after talking to the actresses you watch the next episodes differently: you start to notice how, from one period to the next, Zhenya changes the way she moves, the rhythm of her speech and even her breathing. I’ve checked this personally.

You can read the piece at this link.

15 September 2026

I deliberately let a good few episodes of Cold pile up so I could watch them in one go, and it paid off.

The series, and Lyubov Aksyonova’s performance in particular, has a kind of immersive pull that knocks you off your feet. I lost touch with reality during the first two episodes, and I lost it again watching all the ones that followed back to back.

All thanks to what I would call “the aura of a thick novel”. This series is nothing like the usual alternation of events and their consequences for various characters. Cold feels like a vast tapestry woven from a multitude of threads in different shades. First, the “threads” never get lost, though it may be many years before they meet again. Second, there is anything you like among them except black and white. Third, while this “tapestry” is being woven you would do well to stock up on patience — just like the heroine, Zhenya. And the “embroidery” is so dense that you feel a physical urge to “reread” the series before it has even reached its finale. I really can’t wait for the last episode.

22 September 2026

Rounding off the conversation about Cold

I took part in a survey run by colleagues at Kinopoisk: we swapped views on the series finale. I’m satisfied, as my 9/10 rating makes clear, but what seems especially important to me is the room left for the viewer’s hope in the closing shots. What matters is not so much that Aksyonova’s character had some kind of choice and some kind of decision was made as the reminder that, after the most terrible scrapes, a person still has some power over themselves, even when the outside world can’t be controlled. Lyubov talked to me about this in our interview — it’s great that this is exactly what you feel once you’ve finished watching.

I’d also recommend reading my colleagues’ opinions: each of us took a liking to (or, on the contrary, remembered for the wrong reasons) something different. And I invite you into the comments — I’m sure there’s plenty to discuss ⬇️

Cold: An Immersion That Knocks You Off Your Feet

In short

“Cold” is a 2026 series directed by Aleksey Kazakov. The series, and Lyubov Aksyonova’s performance in particular, has an immersive effect that somehow knocks you off your feet.

BIBetsy Isakova·16 July 2026·updated 22 September 2026

About the series

Title
Cold · Холод
Director
Aleksey Kazakov
Year
2026

16 July 2026

From today you can watch Cold (Kholod), a chilling series with Lyubov Aksyonova.

When I watched the first episode, I literally lost track of time: from about the fifth minute I was simply clutching my head, trying not to start voicing the scale of the plot catastrophe out loud in unprintable language. The sheer number of misfortunes that befall Zhenya, Aksyonova’s heroine, at times really makes you forget to breathe. The very case where, while watching a series, you are not once tempted to check your phone.

At the same time Zhenya is so resilient, enduring and patient that it is hard to deny her sympathy, and the desire to root for her is the main motivation to watch to the end. Every scene shows very directly what kind of real deprivation is at stake (moral and physical), without sparing the viewer and without trying, as it were, to “hide” the uncomfortable moments of the story outside the frame. A series of rare sincerity about the powerlessness of the little man before the unjust blows of fate.

6 August 2026

In a recent interview with Sobchak (the Russian TV journalist), Lyubov Aksyonova told a story that is hard to forget: her father spent 4 years in a penal colony and then proved his innocence in court. She was 19. Now she plays Zhenya in Cold, a drama from Ivi, START and Wink (Russian streaming services) about a woman unjustly convicted over a car accident. When you know this, you look at the role quite differently.

Today my interview with Lyubov Aksyonova, Linda Lapiņš and Alexandra Babaskina came out in The Blueprint. We talked about trauma, how it breaks you, makes you grow up and sometimes becomes a source of strength, and about how to stay human through all these trials.

Inside there is a lot that the trailer will not tell you: why Aksyonova, for the first time in her career, drew up a detailed map of her character and why the early Zhenya went by the code name “Snegurochka” (the Snow Maiden); Lapiņš’s real story of a girl whose fate seemed predetermined but which ended at Yale University; and why Sasha Babaskina is sure that children are sometimes stronger than adults.

The piece turned out insanely beautiful, not only in its ideas but visually: special love to the photographer Maxim Tarakanov and the whole team

As always, I picked out the quote that is key for me; it was voiced by Lyubov Aksyonova ⤵️

Cold is a story about how, even from the most terrible ordeal, a person always has the chance to come out towards love and to lean on it. Love can turn out to be stronger than the desire to destroy. And only love can really defeat death. And that, probably, is the most important thing for me.

By the way, after the conversation with the actresses the following episodes look different: you begin to notice how Zhenya changes her body language, the rhythm of her speech and even her breathing from one period to the next. I checked it myself.

You can read the piece at the link.

15 September 2026

I deliberately saved up more episodes of Cold to watch them in one go, and I was not wrong.

The series, and Lyubov Aksyonova’s performance in particular, has an immersive effect that somehow knocks you off your feet. I lost my sense of connection with reality during the first two episodes, and I lost it again watching all the following episodes back to back.

All thanks to what I would call “the aura of a thick novel”. This series is nothing like the typical alternation of events and consequences for various characters. Cold gives the impression of a vast canvas woven from a multitude of threads in different shades. First, the “threads” are never lost, even if they may meet again many years later. Second, among them there is anything you like except black and white. Third, while this “canvas” is being woven, it is worth stocking up on patience, just like the heroine, Zhenya. And the “embroidery” is so dense that you feel a physical urge to “reread” the series even before the finale. I am really looking forward to the last episode.

22 September 2026

Wrapping up the conversation about Cold

I took part in a poll by colleagues from Kinopoisk: we exchanged opinions on the series’ finale. I am pleased, which shows in my rating of 9/10 too, but what seems especially important to me is the gap left for the viewer’s hope in the closing frames. What matters is not even that Aksyonova’s heroine had some choice and some decision was made, but rather the reminder that after the most terrible ordeals a person retains some power over themselves, even if the outside world cannot be controlled. Lyubov told me about this in our interview, and it is great that exactly this is felt by the end of the viewing.

I separately recommend reading my colleagues’ opinions: each of us took a liking to (or, conversely, remembered with a negative context) something different. And I invite you to the comments; I am sure there is plenty to discuss ⬇️

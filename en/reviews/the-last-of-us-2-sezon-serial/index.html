The Last of Us, Season 2: The Beauty of Ruins Has Beaten the Dialogue Again

In short

“The Last of Us” is a 2023 series directed by Neil Druckmann, Peter Hoar. The beauty of the ruins has beaten the dialogue again, so that even solid suspense loses conviction.

BIBetsy Isakova·20 April 2025·updated 1 June 2025

About the series

Title
The Last of Us
Director
Neil Druckmann, Peter Hoar
Year
2023

20 April 2025

The opening episode of season 2 of The Last of Us is simply a combination of the uncombinable.

The first and clearest feeling is that over the 5 years that have passed in the world of the series, Ellie seems to have lost some of her brains. Seriously, this is a character completely incomparable with the first season, and it is not about growing up: vivid displays of teenage rebellion at the age of 19 provoke bewilderment at the very least. Naturally, such a character transformation sets the necessary level of intrigue, but as a viewer I am none the better for it.

Despite Ellie’s wild energy, the whole episode proceeds sluggishly, even for exposition. This creates another dissonance, which, paradoxically, really whets your interest and desire to keep watching. And so, on contrasts, the new chapter of this story begins.

27 April 2025

After the expository first episode, The Last of Us continued with a brisk invasion of zombies and dramatic changes in the characters’ storylines, which is what we are all here for

The second episode is striking for the lifelike chaos of everything that happens, when before a fateful day people quarrelled by chance, missed each other by chance, and met when it was already too late. However, the overwhelming majority of meetings in this episode bring grief to at least one of those meeting: in this one sees the season’s dominant principle of “man is a wolf to man”. The rest of the season will obviously go on trying to deal with the chain of violence Joel unwittingly set in motion, and exactly what the series’ creators will do with the game’s resolution is doubly interesting.

In my view, the first two episodes should have been released together at once: they make up a harmonious set-up for the season with a variety of storylines ⬅️ But we have what we have, and now, in the third episode, we should expect, I think, a drop in pace. Ellie has a lot to think over, accept and decide for herself before moving on, and in this, incidentally, her situation coincides with that of the whole town, which has just survived a terrible attack by the infected. We wait for flashbacks with Pedro Pascal, place our bets on how quickly the characters will come out of their post-traumatic stupor, and keep watching.

4 May 2025

Well, the third episode of The Last of Us turned out, as I expected, to be a sharp drop in pace.

Even the friends’ journey to Seattle is built not so much as a dynamic trip as a meditative drive through the locations transferred from the game to the series. The same can be said of the scene where Ellie looks around Joel’s empty house: in it I see not only a farewell to someone close to her but also a demonstration of the calm, domestic artistic world that remains in the past for the characters.

Nevertheless, the season is approaching its middle, and, given such stretching of the plot, it becomes clear why the series was renewed for a 3rd season)

11 May 2025

The second season of The Last of Us has passed its midpoint, and it seems that something is about to begin other than the positioning of forces, of which we have already seen plenty.

Seattle has finally been reached, the girls’ secrets from each other have come out, the enemies and ill-wishers are clear. Before the end of the season I would like to catch at least a glimpse of the meeting with the adversaries and the long-awaited revenge.

There are a couple of questions about the universe. Ellie first, with the confidence of a seasoned reader of specialist literature, tells the psychologist everything she wants to hear from a traumatised girl on the road to recovery. But a couple of episodes later the same Ellie cannot for the life of her figure out what the rainbow symbols on the walls of the abandoned city are?)

Such knowledge of the psychological topics fashionable in our world, unnatural for a post-apocalyptic context, on the one hand, and complete ignorance of cultural symbols from literally 20 years before (in their world), look strange to say the least. On the other hand, such specifics of the screen world are intriguing in their own way and make you keep watching.

18 May 2025

The fifth episode of The Last of Us 2 is frustrating, and I already regret that HBO stretched the game over 2 seasons.

The beauty of the ruins has beaten the dialogue again, so that even solid suspense loses conviction. Obviously most of the shortcomings are linked to the decision to split the second part of the game into two full seasons: it seems to me this greatly lowered the pace of the narrative and damaged character development.

But there is still hope for the couple of final episodes. First, thanks to the cruelty Ellie demonstrated. In my post on the first episode of the season I wrote that over the past 5 years Ellie seemed to have lost some of her brains, but now I see that this is more than compensated by determination and principle, as in the good old first season. In the scene of the brutal interrogation of Abby’s comrade-in-arms, I finally found it interesting to watch what was happening, even if the whole scene unfolded in a dark, utterly unpicturesque (unlike the rest of the post-apocalyptic world) corner of a basement.

Second, an impressive line of the hopelessness of people as a species is emerging. In the face of a growing danger of complete extinction (the infection now spreads in the form of spores that poison the air, and the infected are evolving rapidly), humanity continues to splinter into small warring groups, finding reasons purely at the level of principles. Perhaps it would be worth returning to the theme of inventing a cure after all?

Third, a spoiler flashback in the final frames hints that episode 6 may consist entirely of memories. There we will have Pedro Pascal, the unwinding of the plot intrigue and, I hope, gripping dialogue.

25 May 2025

In episode 6, The Last of Us 2 literally came alive, and that is encouraging ahead of the final episode.

The whole episode I delighted in the realistic dialogue, the duo of Ellie and Joel and the growing context (it had been lacking until now). But it is obvious that this is a brief breather before the season finale, which is supposed to leave a powerful intrigue hanging while we wait for the next episodes.

By the way, the episode very rarely shows Ellie and Joel in the same frame, apparently to reflect their estrangement before the fateful quarrel. I caught a rare exception for the post.

1 June 2025

The final episode of The Last of Us 2 is flesh of the flesh of the whole season: ambiguous, debatable and in places infuriating in its illogic.

It is already customary that with Joel’s disappearance from the action, the dialogue sags noticeably. But this episode made me yell at the screen for a different reason. In the scene where Ellie runs into Abby’s comrades and accidentally kills both of them, one particular moment bothered me. The pregnant young woman, dying, asks Ellie to make an incision in her belly and take out the baby, clearly saying where and how to cut. In response Ellie switches on panic (🤷‍♀️), does not even try to do anything (🤦‍♀️) and with a shocked look (as if it were unexpected) watches the young woman die. Let us suppose that is simply Ellie’s character and nothing can be done about it. But in a world where you can be devoured by an infected at any second, the reaction should be up to it. And this scene shows us only how easily the logic of the world collapses for the sake of “complicating” the inner conflict of the heroine, so contradictory, misunderstood by everyone and lonely.

The final cliffhanger with the gunshot does, of course, leave the intrigue hanging, but after the scene described above it no longer impresses so much. It is good that while we wait for the next season we will have a couple of years to think it over.

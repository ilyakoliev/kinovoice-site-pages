Hard to Be a God: A First Episode Without the Bombast, and a Finale Worth Reaching

2 September 2026

The first episode of Hard to Be a God (Trudno byt bogom) has an absolutely wonderful aura: none of the knock-you-off-your-feet bombast that is traditional for Russian fantasy.

This interpretation of the Strugatskys does not claim to be the fundamental, final truth; on the contrary, you can tell it was made by contemporaries for contemporary viewers. Sergey Bezrukov’s Don Condor every now and then lets slip the smirk of Belov from Brigada. Nikita Kologrivy, too, does what he does well, and in the very first episode he is already squabbling with the local king in the raised tones of Koshchey from The Boy’s Word.

And so, in small details and shades of intonation, every actor is, to my mind, 100% in the right place: Viktoria Isakova, Irina Pautova, Lev Zulkarnaev, Ravshana Kurkova, Alexander Demidov and many, many others. The audience giggled approvingly when screenwriter Andrey Zolotarev, playing the treasurer, nodded with great decorum in a small scene of the first episode. I wonder whether his character will get a big story arc?)

A particular pleasure was Aleksandar Radojičić as Don Rumata, the protagonist through whose eyes we begin our acquaintance with the world of Arkanar. Unlike the actors listed above, he carries no fixed associations for the mass audience, and that is great, because it means we cannot predict his character: a genuine intrigue. Besides, in real life Aleksandar has the sweetest Serbian accent, and the one thing I did not manage to work out while watching was whether he had been dubbed over entirely, or whether a slight accent had been kept as a sign that the hero, even by the standards of the setting, has arrived from some very, very distant land. To my mind, it would be great to convey the hero’s otherness through the actor’s natural accent.

Also, colleagues at Wink (the Russian streaming service) have made a very informative (and entertaining) long read on the universe of Hard to Be a God: you can take a quiz to find out which Observer you are and leaf through the characters’ dossiers. Alongside watching the series (which, as you have gathered, I plan to keep doing), I will be reading the Strugatskys’ novel and watching Aleksei German’s feature-length version: I want to immerse myself fully in this metaverse.

18 October 2026

Getting to the finale of Hard to Be a God turned out to be harder than I thought, but it was all worth it.

First, the difficulties: in my view, from somewhere around the middle the series becomes cramped in its own cluttered world. The rhythm falters, the episodes become unevenly packed, and so viewer engagement suffers too (mine, at any rate).

On the other hand, Don Rumata as played by Aleksandar Radojičić was a pleasure. He is detached enough for an Observer and at the same time sincerely interested in gaining the experience of mortals. This both saves him and lets him down; here everything depends on how you feel about the finale. In my opinion, it never hurts to remember at what price the passage from dark times to light is won.

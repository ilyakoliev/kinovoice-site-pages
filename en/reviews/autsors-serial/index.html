Outsource: The Finale, and I Have Not Enjoyed Series This Much in a Long Time

In short

“Outsource” is a 2025 series directed by Dushan Gligorov. It is good that the story has partly come full circle: now Kostya Volkov will go off to a new job at another penal colony and stir things up there.

BIBetsy Isakova·13 February 2025·updated 27 March 2025

About the series

Title
Outsource · Аутсорс
Director
Dushan Gligorov
Year
2025

13 February 2025

It is pleasing that practically at the start of the series year such a cool story as Outsource (Autsors) comes out: a series with Ivan Yankovsky as Kostya, an enterprising prison warder who in the mid-1990s decides to sell victims’ relatives the chance to execute the killer with their own hands. This plot spine is surrounded by spine-chillingly terrible stories of the hero’s colleagues and other inhabitants of the town, which makes the series’ structure rich! In this respect I like this non-main poster of the series: Kostya is clearly going to stir up some volcano of consequences.

Judging by the first episodes, Yankovsky’s character serves as an infernal otherworldly force that gradually brings out in the locals hidden traits they had not even suspected, asking awkward but very simple questions and offering not the most moral but direct solutions. You can enjoy this character largely as an extension and continuation of Yankovsky’s work as Raskolnikov, only a Raskolnikov who feels he has the right and gives no thought to repentance.

I hope Outsource will set the quality bar for other projects this year too, because so far it is the strongest thing of 2025.

10 March 2025

How brilliantly details work in Outsource!

Colleagues noted the prosecutor’s house-warming scene, but I was struck by the transformation of Natasha’s mother, played by Karina Razumovskaya. All the time she was giving her daughter destructive advice from the position of “the main thing is to hang on to a good man”, behaving not as a mother but as an auntie personifying public opinion, she wore her hat without taking it off: a symbol of closedness, insincerity, distance from her daughter’s feelings.

But when the irreparable happened in episode 5 (Natasha, after an abortion done on her mother’s advice, ended up in intensive care and will never be able to have children), Natasha’s mother at last took off her protective armour of coldness and demandingness towards her daughter. For the first time in the series (this did not happen even at the wedding!) she appeared before her daughter without the hat and without the swagger, even though it happened unforgivably late. A small thing, but the effect on the viewer is colossal.

13 March 2025

At some point Outsource began turning into House of the Dragon in the number of its intrigues, and my soul demanded an episode-by-episode breakdown.

1️⃣ I still have doubts: does Ivan Yankovsky’s character exist at all? He is some kind of incorporeal shadow that manages to be here, there and everywhere, persuading anyone he meets with the efficiency of a hypnotist. I would not be surprised if in the end he vanished, like Tyler Durden, leaving the jailers with a pile of insoluble problems. Especially after the line “And who could do anything to me anyway?”…

2️⃣ The poster with the women turned out to be very apt: judging by episode 6, the ball is passing to their side, and now interesting twists are to be expected precisely from the female half of the characters. Natasha’s mother has put her hat back on, though authority has, on the contrary, grown only in Natasha herself, which makes her all the more interesting to watch. In general, all the female characters here are so top-notch that, despite minimal screen time, I am ready to praise them separately, beyond my general admiration for the cast.

3️⃣With every week the episodes fly by faster and faster; given the number of untangled plot threads, in the remaining two episodes an ultimate resolution awaits us with answers to all the questions at once (and answers that are surprisingly simple, as always with Yankovsky’s character). Literally on the principle of “seven troubles, one answer”.

21 March 2025

The seventh episode of Outsource seems to have been squeezed too much (in both running time and events), and on the whole looks like a quiet break for both sides of the conflict to prepare their strategy. Perhaps such swiftness is needed for the inevitable approach to the terrible (no doubt it will be exactly that) resolution, but as a viewer I would of course like this story to continue endlessly.

1️⃣ The women, as I thought, have taken the situation into their own hands. And, surprisingly, the most harm turned out to come from the mothers, Natasha’s and Roma’s. The first brought into the house a cutthroat worse than our main characters: the prison governor. As a result he overheard Natasha’s revelations about the money, which she had for some reason (Lord, after all her mother’s abuse and the story with the abortion?!) actually brought there. And the second was so impressed by Volkov’s warnings about “the old prostitute” that she stashed her son’s money away for good, nobody knows where (I suspect that in her simplicity she might have handed it to that same Kostya Volkov “for safekeeping”).

2️⃣ Kostya Volkov’s daughter, it seems, does have a soul after all. If Ivan Yankovsky’s character sniffs out that his daughter is now lying to him too (and he has already figured everything out from the suitcase and the dirty boots), it will be interesting to see what fate befalls her.

3️⃣ Speaking of Volkov. There was so little of him in this episode that I am more and more convinced he never existed at all. Nevertheless, in favour of his existence is the prison governor’s order to deal with Volkov. I think that will be the main attraction of the final episode: who ends up dealing with whom.

4️⃣ The prosecutor’s wife underwent a very interesting character transformation in this episode. I always had a special kind of sympathy for her, and now, for the brilliant self-organisation of the prison staff’s wives, I respect her even more. A pity only that, it seems, it will lead to roughly nothing.

27 March 2025

Outsource is over. I have not enjoyed series this much in a long time (since Give a Show!); to put it briefly, it is simply something else (I am as pleased as Mila Ershova in this still). And at more length, here are a few thoughts:

  • I kept hoping that Volkov did not exist, but the creators did something cooler and made him immortal! I do not remember when I was as glad for any film character as when Volkov turned out to be alive. (All right, it is simpler: either the convict doctor gave the wrong recipe for the cocktail, which is why he laughed so hard at the end of the episode, or the prison doctor got it mixed up, or the potion simply worked on the gravely ill, and Yankovsky’s healthy character survived it).

  • By the way, I am also glad that Volkov did not carry out the final shooting with his own hands: that would have gone against his character. And now it is also clear what happened at Volkov’s previous job and why he was transferred: obviously his former colleagues also failed his uncomplicated test of their mettle.

  • Hooray, they showed, if only for a few seconds, the collapse of our friends’ business because of the abolition of the death penalty. On the one hand, as the memes say, it is gratifying: the death penalty went away together with the people who made it an instrument of profit. On the other hand, what else, if we hypothetically continued the series, could prison warders sell to the powerful after the abolition of the death penalty? Demand creates supply, as we know, so inventors like Volkov will probably never die out.

  • The women, as usual, are on fire. In this episode specifically the heroines of Mila Ershova (her heroine’s walk with the Raffaellos has already become a meme) and Darya Kotreleva (a spectacular escape from everyone gave her heroine perhaps the happiest resolution). It is very good that all the female characters, though they were supporting ones, received in the finale some clear full stop or trajectory of future development; supporting heroines do not always get that attention.

  • It is good that the story has partly come full circle: now Kostya Volkov will go off to a new job at another penal colony and stir things up there. This is even better than a standard finale: now a second season of this story is definitely not needed (unless in anthology format).

The Giant Ants of Novosibirsk: A Graphic Novel That Lulls Your Vigilance

In short

“The Giant Ants of Novosibirsk” is a 2023 book by Vitaly Terletsky. Stas, a 22-year-old unemployed man, tells his friends in the minutest detail about his childhood memories: the giant ants of Novosibirsk.

BIBetsy Isakova·16 December 2023

About the book

Title
The Giant Ants of Novosibirsk · Гигантские муравьи Новосибирска
Author
Vitaly Terletsky
Year
2023

i not only talk but sometimes write too (and not only about films but about books as well!)

for example, with colleagues from Kinopoisk and Bookmate we put together the book highlights of the year, and I gave my vote almost without hesitation to the graphic novel I had just (at the time the selection was being compiled) read, by Vitaly Terletsky and Evgenia Chashchina, Gigantskie muravyi Novosibirska (“The Giant Ants of Novosibirsk”):

Stas, a 22-year-old unemployed man, tells his friends in the minutest detail about his childhood memories: the giant ants of Novosibirsk. Only Stas is from Kirov and has never been to Novosibirsk. Around this conflict between memory and facts, in an elegant chaos, flash the adventures of the Decembrist Muravyov-Apostol, love, sculptures of giant dinosaurs and the search for one’s own “self”. And the protagonist himself resembles Solovyov’s Bananan from Assa dropped into the world of the film Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind.

Cinematic and escapist, but far from as lightweight as it seems at first glance, Terletsky and Chashchina’s graphic novel lulls your vigilance with rainbow watercolour. And for its finale it offers a hard choice (one that has gained relevance in the last couple of years) between soul-saving but false memories and looking hopeless reality in the face. And, it seems, for the hero the choice is obvious.

before settling on my recommendation for good, I also thought about Alexei Ivanov’s Broneparokhody (“Armoured Steamers”); I sincerely recommend this book (which is also in our selection!) no less than The Giant Ants; it definitely made my top book impressions of the year)

but Broneparokhody lost to The Ants on one subjective criterion of mine, code-named “my jaw dropped and I had to pick it up”.

all the films and books (and pieces of music, and [insert as appropriate]) that I am ready to remember among the best (both of the year and of my life) must make me feel roughly like that, otherwise the road to the podium is closed) and my tastes are peculiar, so completely unpredictable things sometimes meet the subjective criterion, but that is another story…

in general I have been reading Terletsky for a long time, and I strongly recommend not only the fresh novel but also such time-tested (and reread) things as Russkie obedinyayutsya s krokodilami i napadayut na Evropu (“The Russians Join Forces with the Crocodiles and Attack Europe”) and Padenie Yeltsina s mosta (“Yeltsin Falls off a Bridge”); here, it seems, the titles themselves are the best recommendation

Cashier: Four Heroines at Opposite Poles of Control over Their Lives

In short

“Cashier” is a 2025 series directed by Boris Akopov. What strikes me most is how the 4 main heroines stand at opposite poles when it comes to control over their own lives and the lives of those around them.

27 October 2025

Now here is some news that makes me happy: from 13 November, Wink (a Russian streaming service) will be showing the series Cashier (Obnalshchik, “The Cash-Out Man”), a twisty story from Boris Akopov.

The series is set in 2013 and centres on the relationship between a father, Mikhail, and his son, Lyosha. After a string of setbacks, the two of them start cashing out money for dangerous people. The threat of a prison sentence, murders, betrayals: this is their new everyday life.

The cast includes Alexander Yatsenko, Elena Nikolaeva and Fyodor Kudryashov, and it was shot in the Russian Far East, which means that besides the crime plot we are also in for landscapes like nowhere else (I love that region dearly myself, and I can vouch for its beauty). The lead, Mikhail, played by Yatsenko, looks to be my favourite type, the “bad good man”; here is what the actor himself says:

Mikhail, like me, isn’t exactly on first-name terms with pedagogy and parenting. Still, he tries to find common ground with his son, to reach an understanding. You know how it goes: you do one wrong thing, and because of it your whole life starts heading the wrong way. And then it’s very hard to rewind, to admit you were wrong, to apologise and start changing something… Our series is about exactly that: how a person makes a mistake and tries to steer his way out of it. Mikhail isn’t a very good man or father, but his love for his son and his wife keeps him from turning into a criminal for good

Adding it to my watchlist, and waiting.

10 November 2025

Cashier treats us to character posters and a trailer (with a certain Daring vibe)

What’s new is the premiere date, which has moved to 15 November. We’ll be seeing the story of a father and son who pull off complicated financial schemes for none-too-pleasant clients as early as the end of this week.

By the way, on the poster for Igor Mirkurbanov’s character, who goes by the Fish King, the lamp in the foreground is exactly like the one I have at home. Clearly, the series must be watched by its light and no other.

19 November 2025

So far Cashier (Obnalshchik, “The Cash-Out Man”) looks to me like the sum of Daring (Likhie: crime in the Far East and Elena Nikolaeva as the mother of the family) and Na avtomate (“On Autopilot”, Boris Akopov’s previous series, where young people also got drawn into criminal adventures). But that doesn’t hurt the series one bit!

Among other things, it’s now clear why the poster makes such a point of the hand of Alexander Yatsenko’s character (those who have already seen the first episode will know what I mean). On the whole, the plot unfolds fairly quietly, yet it hooks you and, of course, wins you over. The criminal world here is heavily disguised as ordinary life, which makes sense: this is, after all, not the 1990s but the 2010s, even if hardly the calmest of times. I’m also curious to see where the theme of young people who have got their hands on easy money will lead: the brothers in Na avtomate were teetering on the brink of just that kind of moral choice too.

10 December 2025

Cashier has very skilfully picked up speed by the end of episode 5.

What strikes me most is how the 4 main heroines stand at opposite poles when it comes to control over their own lives and the lives of those around them. Two of them, Olga (Elena Nikolaeva) and Alyona (Sofya Lopunova), are not in charge of their own lives at all. Their existence is a shadow of the plans that Alexander Yatsenko’s and Fyodor Kudryashov’s characters are trying to carry out, and total ignorance of what is actually going on.

The crime bosses Mama (Olga Tumaykina) and Katya (Elena Nesterova), meanwhile, are their inverse. The whole town is in their power, and any lie, if one is ever told, is brutally punished. Quite unlike the situation of the women the two leads love, who are having the wool pulled over their eyes in the most blatant way.

You could even see a growth trajectory in this: perhaps Vostochny’s current crime queens were once in the same position themselves, and it was from precisely that point that they grew to the scale we are shown.

In Boris Akopov’s stories something is always buried deeper than it first appears, so I have great faith in the women’s storylines.

7 January 2026

I’ve finished Cashier and, of course, I’m impressed not so much by the finale as by the development of one particular heroine: Olga, played by Elena Nikolaeva.

Mid-season I wrote that she has no control over her life at all. Towards the end came a 180-degree turn: her weakness became her strength, a silky weapon with a gentle tone of voice. While her husband and son — Alexander Yatsenko’s and Fyodor Kudryashov’s characters, as convincing as ever — acted as outside circumstances and other people’s orders dictated, she simply took matters into her own hands.

Just think: she drove her guard (whose job was to kill the heroine a couple of hours later) to take himself out! I’m thrilled: Boris Akopov has come up with a heroine who showed up every gangster in the Far East with a single scene. She may not be much of a psychologist, but as a moral annihilator she delivered in full :)

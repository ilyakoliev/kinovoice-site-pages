Cashier: Four Heroines at Opposite Poles of Controlling Their Lives

In short

“Cashier” is a 2025 series directed by Boris Akopov. What is especially striking is the opposite poles at which the 4 main heroines stand when it comes to controlling their own lives and the lives of those around them.

BIBetsy Isakova·19 November 2025·updated 7 January 2026

About the series

Title
Cashier · Обнальщик
Director
Boris Akopov
Year
2025

19 November 2025

So far Cashier (Obnalshchik, “The Cash-Out Man”) looks to me like the sum of Daring (Likhie: crime in the Far East and Elena Nikolaeva as the mother of the family) and Na avtomate (“On Autopilot”, Boris Akopov’s previous project, where young people were also drawn into criminal adventures). But this does the series no harm at all!

Among other things, it has become clear why the poster puts such emphasis on the hand of Alexander Yatsenko’s character (those who have already seen the first episode will understand me). On the whole, the plot develops fairly quietly, yet grippingly, and, of course, wins you over. The criminal world here is heavily disguised as ordinary life, which is understandable: after all, this is not the 1990s but the 2010s, even if not the calmest of times. Besides, it is interesting to see what the theme of young people who have got their hands on easy money will lead to: the brothers in Na avtomate were also balancing before exactly that kind of moral choice.

10 December 2025

Cashier has very competently picked up speed by the end of episode 5.

What is especially striking is the opposite poles at which the 4 main heroines stand when it comes to controlling their own lives and the lives of those around them. Two of them, Olga (Elena Nikolaeva) and Alyona (Sofya Lopunova), are not their own mistresses at all. Their life is the shadow of plans that the characters of Alexander Yatsenko and Fyodor Kudryashov are trying to carry out, and complete ignorance of what is going on at all.

Meanwhile the crime bosses Mama (Olga Tumaykina) and Katya (Elena Nesterova) are their reverse projection. The whole town is in their power, and any lie, if it happens at all, is cruelly punished. Not at all the situation the heroes’ beloveds find themselves in, with noodles hanging blatantly from their ears.

Perhaps one can even see a growth trajectory in this: maybe the current crime queens of Vostochny were once in that position too, and it was from exactly this point that they grew to the scale we are shown.

In Boris Akopov’s stories there is always something hidden deeper than it seems at first glance, so I have great faith in the female storylines.

7 January 2026

I have finished Cashier and am, of course, impressed not so much by the finale as by the development of one particular heroine, Olga as played by Elena Nikolaeva.

In the middle of the season I wrote that she has no control over her life at all. Towards the end there was a 180-degree turn: weakness became her strength, an insinuating weapon with a gentle intonation. While her husband and son, the characters of Alexander Yatsenko and Fyodor Kudryashov, who lost none of their conviction, acted according to external circumstances and other people’s orders, she simply took the situation into her own hands.

To think she drove her guard (whose task was to kill the heroine a couple of hours later) to self-destruct! I am delighted: Boris Akopov came up with a heroine who outclassed all the gangsters of the Far East in a single scene. Maybe she is not much of a psychologist, but as a moral annihilator she worked at full capacity)

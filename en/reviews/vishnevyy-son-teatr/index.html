Cherry Dream at a39: A New Gogol Center Has Appeared

In short

“Vishnyovy son” is a 2025 stage production directed by Oleg Glushkov. While watching, you feel that the production is, as it were, speaking the same language as you, without forgetting its source, and that is worth a great deal

BIBetsy Isakova·11 August 2025

About the production

Title
Vishnyovy son · Вишнёвый сон
Director
Oleg Glushkov
Year
2025

“A new Gogol Center has appeared!”, I thought in the first minutes of Vishnyovy son («Вишнёвый сон», “Cherry Dream”) at a39 (the Moscow theatre).

And really, it turned out to be a superb piece of work in terms of combining Chekhov’s original The Cherry Orchard with contemporary stage design. I had missed this since the production of The Misanthrope at the Gogol Center (the Moscow theatre closed in 2022).

While watching, you feel that the production is, as it were, speaking the same language as you, without forgetting its source, and that is worth a great deal. Everything from the design and costumes to the music and the fit between the actors and their roles is simply a 100% hit. I have a weakness for the use of techno music in theatre, there is nothing I can do about it, but how apt it turned out to be here: the sound of the breaking string that we deserve. And “The Winner Takes It All”, sung towards the end, is, whichever way you look at it, the most concise and laconic musical embodiment of Lopakhin’s tragedy (at first slyly disguised by director Oleg Glushkov as a dramedy).

As for the cast, it is very, very convincing. I want to rewatch every line of Ranevskaya as played by Serafima Goshchanskaya, every monologue of Petya Trofimov as played by Sergei Kirpichonok, every affectation of Dunyasha as played by Elizabeth Damsker and, especially, every scene of Lopakhin as played by Ilya Ozolin. I am ready to shower the theatre’s company with compliments endlessly: every one of these guys is simply inimitable, and the sooner they start being cast more in our films and series, the better (colleagues, take note!)

And for now we await Ilya Ozolin’s big dramatic role in Bakuradze’s Lermontov: after he lived (my tongue will not say “played”) Lopakhin on stage, my expectations have grown.

The Black Countess: A Mix of the Aesthetics of Of Freaks and Men and Kholop

In short

“Chyornaya grafinya” is a 2026 series directed by Ilya Kulikov, Ivan Korvin. What we have is seemingly a colossal handmade performance for one man, and seemingly a bad dream about captives bound by a vicious mutual guarantee.

29 March 2026

After the screening of The Black Countess (Chyornaya grafinya) at Original+ (the Russian series showcase) I honestly hoped that a couple of episodes would come out at once on premiere day on Okko (the Russian streaming service). The first episode, though incredibly packed, breaks off at the most interesting point. Here is what I wrote about the plot:

The present day. In a richly decorated country estate a play is being vigorously rehearsed. The director, Timur (Yuri Chursin), never tires of reminding the actors that the client has paid a million dollars. The sole spectator (Kirill Käro) arrives, and he is impressed neither by the simple-hearted joy of the actress playing the “niece”, nor by the society talk in the style of 19th-century plays, nor even by the voyeuristic crack in the wall of the room allotted to him, which allows him to spy on the ladies’ bathing. The fire in his eyes appears only after one of the actors really dies. The gun turned out to be loaded with a live round, and the carefully rehearsed shot proved fatal. The strangeness does not end there: first the frightened troupe discovers that the satellite phone (the only means of communication in a deliberately created offline space) is broken, and then learns that the mansion where the show is taking place is cursed. According to legend, it once belonged to a countess who, having gone mad after her husband’s death, tormented the peasants until they walled her up in the building. Since then, so the tale goes, only death and disease dwell there

But only one episode came out, and that, of course, is the best way to win the viewer’s impatience for the second. Most interesting of all is how the mix of the aesthetics of Of Freaks and Men (Alexei Balabanov’s 1998 film) and Kholop (the 2019 hit comedy about a rich man re-educated in a staged 19th century) will develop. What we have is seemingly a colossal handmade performance for one man, and seemingly a bad dream about captives bound by a vicious mutual guarantee.

15 April 2026

I am continuing to watch The Black Countess, and by the end of the third episode, of course, an expectation arises of some new mechanism to keep the intrigue going.

We have already seen dead characters, and the surviving ones’ hypotheses do not look plausible at all. A certain feeling of going round in circles arises, which I hope will be broken by the events of the next episode, because the story has very great potential.

Incidentally, in terms of immersion in the backstage of an actor’s life, The Black Countess distantly resembles Aktrisy (“Actresses”), another project about the fate of actors, only not at all mystical and firmly tied to the present day. It remains to guess which way The Black Countess will lean more.

The character posters give away nicely who will last longer than the others in this meat grinder.

4 May 2026

The deeper into the forest, the less I understand what is going on in The Black Countess at all.

The killings of characters have stopped being the main engine of the plot, replaced by a sorting through of suspects. This already gives the proceedings a new angle for the closing episodes, but at the same time, together with the other ingredients, it completely blurs the genre of the series. In other words, it knocks the ground out from under the viewer’s feet. In some respects that is even good, but there are nuances.

The Black Countess, the legend of whose fate has been recounted more than once during the series, has acquired a storyline of her own. Now we see, as it were retrospectively, a visualisation of her whole story with her husband, children and peasants. And this repetition seems to slow down noticeably the storylines of the present-day characters (though only a few of them are left). The purpose of this duplication of the legend on screen is not entirely clear, but I will very much hope that in the end it pays off worthily.

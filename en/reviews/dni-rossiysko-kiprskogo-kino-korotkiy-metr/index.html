Five Russian Shorts from the Days of Russian-Cypriot Cinema

In short

I watched almost the entire short film programme at the Days of Russian-Cypriot Cinema festival, and I want to share 5 of the Russian films.

BIBetsy Isakova·27 September 2026

Yesterday I watched almost the entire short film programme at the Days of Russian-Cypriot Cinema festival, and I want to share 5 of the Russian films with you. If you get the chance, watch them too, they are wonderful.

  • Mama prosila ne vzryvat meteorit (“Mum Asked Me Not to Blow Up the Meteorite”) is a charming story about a boy from a snowbound village who befriends a meteorite and decides to help it gather all the fragments that have broken off. Enchanting magical realism (by now the trademark of the studio Red Pepper Film) and a lead actor who holds your attention 100%, Yaroslav Golovnev.

  • Kholodny dush (“Cold Shower”) is a compact story about second chances for yourself and for others. The forty-year-old heroine is a single mother who works at the sorting depot of an unnamed online marketplace and, accordingly, is not rolling in money. At work she has a subordinate: a kidult pure and simple, always late but incredibly sweet, with a charming smile, a thermos of fragrant coffee and his signature sandwiches at the ready. How could you not fall in love, especially if it is mutual? But the guy trails problems of his own, and they will have to deal with them together.

  • Kak my deda provozhali (“How We Saw Grandad Off”) is another Red Pepper Film production, which, incidentally, won the festival’s main award. A family at loggerheads travels to bury the grandfather; since his passing, relations between the generations have gone completely sour. Only a miracle can reconcile the parties to the conflict, and it will be a somewhat unusual one. My sincere admiration for the light pouring through the screen and for the most appetising vatrushki (sweet cheese buns) in contemporary Russian cinema.

  • Marusya: the USSR, shortages, ration cards. Marusya, a thief (an incredible performance by Alexandra Bolshakova), is trying to carry an expensive chandelier out of a flat so she can go on the run to the Black Sea. It turns out that the flat is home to a naive mother with a baby, holy simplicity itself, who does not even understand that the father has abandoned them. Marusya faces a difficult moral choice, and watching it is a real psychological drama.

  • Vremya kormit golubey (“Time to Feed the Pigeons”) is another short about the wise, the good and the eternal, in which the only thing that threw me was the hero’s nonchalance in the face of expulsion from university, unrealistic by the standards of 2026. To cheer up his grandmother, her student grandson recreates a pigeon-feeding scene beneath her windows and, it seems, achieves his goal

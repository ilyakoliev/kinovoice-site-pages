Lesson: A Teen Drama That Unwinds Its Plot in Reverse

In short

“Lesson” is a 2025 series directed by Sergey Filatov. The story becomes ever more deeply a teen drama: with bullying, emotional dependencies and parental overprotection.

BIBetsy Isakova·25 April 2025·updated 13 June 2025

About the series

Title
Lesson · Урок
Director
Sergey Filatov
Year
2025

25 April 2025

I had been waiting a long time for the release of Lesson, (Urok), and not in vain: the first episode is a success.

Yura Borisov, as a rapper who has lost his career, his money and his girlfriend (literally: she died in a car crash and he survived), is, as always, meaningfully silent and looks on kindly (especially at kittens). Which cannot be said of Daniil Vorobyov’s character: looking at him, I recall a creature from Russian literature called the nedotykomka. It is the image of the petty demon sitting inside the protagonist of Fyodor Sologub’s novel The Petty Demon. In other words, some small, insidious and clinging nastiness, at first invisible to the eye. Let us see what comes of it in the next episodes, but I have no doubt about how well Daniil Vorobyov’s possessed characters play.

By all the rules of a pilot episode, the teenagers are also getting a storyline of their own, and it is gratifying that it is led by Oleg Savostyuk, an actor who, to my taste, already enjoys credit of trust on a par with Borisov and Vorobyov. It is unusual that after an already thoroughly adult role in Give a Show! (Dayte shou) he has returned to school themes, but it is a pleasure to see him on screen in any role. By the way, I hope the line of the not-quite-a-tomcat Igor will develop too; they did not put him on the poster for nothing!

On the minus side: I am getting Vietnam flashbacks from the country house where the teenagers hang out. It is the same location as the fateful party in Tsikady (“Cicadas”). Those who know how that series ended will understand my nervous tic.

10 May 2025

I continue watching Lesson and cannot fail to note that Yura Borisov and Daniil Vorobyov in the leads are an excellent engine of interest in a series that has turned out not to be quite about their characters’ brotherly relationship.

The story becomes ever more deeply a teen drama: with bullying, emotional dependencies and parental overprotection. And making such a seemingly very typical and familiar set-up interesting is not that easy. The difference is that here the plot unwinds in reverse order: in the first episode, before we properly knew anyone, we witnessed a shooting (which in Tsikady, for example, was precisely the final chord), and in the following episodes we get to know each teenager more closely. Thanks to this intrigue there is no obtrusive feeling that everything is heading towards something definite; on the contrary, there is a chance to guess for yourself what awaits the characters.

For now I feel sorry to the point of tears for Mila (the girl more often called Ponchik, “Doughnut”, than by her name), because she is completely blind to the manipulations of Artyom, who exploits her affection 100% (Oleg Savostyuk’s astonishingly vile character; such a transformation sends a chill down your spine). What will come of their joint drug escapades is frightening even to imagine. More darkness is added by the sinister transformation of Kostya (Vorobyov) from a harmless pedantic bore into a maniacally focused scoundrel. The portrait he unwrapped piece by piece over a couple of episodes was obviously the last thread of his fragile psychological stability. Now, it seems, the character’s marbles have come off together with the wrapping paper.

By the way, where did the pseudo-tomcat with the kittens go? We have not seen Igor for a while, we miss him!

17 May 2025

A few words on why the series Lesson has a brilliantly written script.

Initially, because of the given context, we expect Yura Borisov’s character to develop, since he is the most troubled guy here. But in fact his appearance serves only as a catalyst for the transformation of Daniil Vorobyov’s character, who at first looks more stable than a school bell timetable.

Not a bit of it: judging by half the season, Borisov is now playing a consistently good guy who, in essence, finished with his problems before the action began, off screen. Whereas Vorobyov is precisely the one acquiring problems. And the longer his brother is around, the faster he accelerates.

So the script is good precisely for this substitution of what is stereotypically expected of the characters with what is realistically convincing: a pleasant unpredictability of plot arises at once.

30 May 2025

The new episode of Lesson in 3 points:

Igoressa is back! At last, if only briefly, we got to enjoy an appearance by this heroine again

The episode’s resolution is not encouraging: for several central characters the remaining episodes risk turning from a teen drama into a crime drama.

Where we were learning to manipulate people, Daniil Vorobyov’s character was teaching. Obviously Konstantin has broken bad, and from here it will only get worse. It is hard even to imagine ways of resolving the accumulated conflicts relatively painlessly for all the innocent.

13 June 2025

The finale of Lesson did not disappoint. From the very beginning it was clear that a tragedy would happen, but it was not obvious whom it would touch. It is great that this intrigue remained unpredictable to the end, and when it was revealed, it worked in a very unconventional yet realistic way.

The “superfluous man” in the person of Anton (Borisov) received a resolution classic for 19th-century Russian literature (but it was still unexpected). And Kostya’s (Vorobyov) final monologue definitively fixed the series in the genre of psychological thriller.

I was separately pleased by the scene of giving away Igoressa’s kittens. First, it is as if it were the fate that meeting Anton gave each of the characters. Second, it is the most eco-friendly working through of grief I have seen on film. Zero hints at a second season, an excellent complete story that you even want to rewatch.

Project Hail Mary: Mass-Market Cinema with Taste and Humanity

In short

“Project Hail Mary” is a 2026 film directed by Phil Lord, Christopher Miller. In short, a practically perfect example of mass-market cinema made very skilfully, but with taste and humanity.

BIBetsy Isakova·24 April 2026

About the film

Title
Project Hail Mary
Director
Phil Lord, Christopher Miller
Year
2026

I have got round to Project Hail Mary, (released in Russia as Proekt “Konets sveta”, “Project Doomsday”) and it is a genuinely, cosmically cool film

The story of a teacher who ends up on a space mission against his will is good in many ways: stunning cinematography and graphics, a lovely Ryan Gosling (I do not like all his roles, but this one is right on target), a convincing script without an excess of melodrama or a slide into overly black humour. By the way, it is also a great adaptation of the novel of the same name by Andy Weir (the author previously wrote The Martian; space is his forte). In short, a practically perfect example of mass-market cinema made very skilfully, but with taste and humanity.

And about the latter I want to write separately. It is obvious, probably, that the film immediately invites comparison with Interstellar: in scale, in its concerns, in the overlaps of the plot. But Interstellar, in my view, is simply an anti-advertisement for the space science-fiction genre compared with Project. The film with Gosling does not build a wall between the action and the viewer out of grandeur and ambition that one must look at exclusively from below and with reverence. The detail of Project, unlike Interstellar, does not require homework on analysing what you have seen in order to understand and remember at least something. And this, surprisingly, does not make the script flat or predictable; on the contrary, it helps you engage and get greater pleasure from watching.

So think about that, as they say. And do watch Project Hail Mary, it is worth it)

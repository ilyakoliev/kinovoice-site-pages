I Love You Phillip Morris: From Sly Frenzy to Penitent Melancholy

About the film

Title
I Love You Phillip Morris
Director
Glenn Ficarra, John Requa
Year
2009

I watched a film that only I and its creators remember.

It is the dramedy I Love You Phillip Morris with Jim Carrey and Ewan McGregor. When you first see this film, you think: why has STS (the Russian TV channel) never shown it? It has Jim Carrey in it, doesn’t it?

But half an hour into watching you understand everything (though this aspect, naturally, is not the main one at all).

The picture is unusually constructed: from the very beginning there is no colossal task or danger facing the hero. We simply watch him try to adapt to a world in which even just living quietly in his own way takes effort, never mind reaching any heights (though he strives for those too).

In vibe it is to some extent like Better Call Saul and Liar Liar with the same Carrey. That is, before us is the most charming trickster, who still pays for every deft manoeuvre with bitter tears. And so we rush about together with the hero from sly frenzy to penitent melancholy, which always works as the most gripping attraction.

Notably, the picture has no firm ending: the hero seems fated to keep wriggling like this all his life. So the drama in this dramedy, in my view, certainly outweighs the comedy (which is always convincing)

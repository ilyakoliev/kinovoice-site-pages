The Restoration of the Udarnik Cinema: Why It Matters

Betsy Isakova·28 July 2024·updated 30 March 2025

28 July 2024

How lovely to see that the Udarnik cinema (a historic Moscow cinema) is genuinely being renovated as a cinema (judging by the signs on the hoarding), and not as a shopping centre housed in a Soviet building! I can’t wait — it stood derelict for so many years.

Why does it matter? I’ve put together a couple of facts:

  • The Udarnik was the first cinema built specifically for showing sound films

  • It was at the Udarnik that the first Moscow International Film Festival (back then still simply the Soviet Film Festival in Moscow) was held

  • Its unique domed roof slid apart to reveal the sky, but only once: on the cinema’s first day of operation in 1931 (then the mechanism jammed, and the roof never opened again). I’d like to believe that after the restoration it will be opened many more times.

30 March 2025

I’m still following the restoration of the Udarnik, and still rejoicing.

Some interesting infographics have appeared, and even renders showing what everything will look like. The revamped Udarnik will have lots of screens, a bookshop, a café and, as far as I understand, the famous domed roof in working (at last!) order.

The renovation will go on until the end of 2027, so in 2028 we’ll surely be able to pick up a couple of tickets for a screening there and wander round a building that’s practically a hundred years old (construction of the Udarnik first began in 1928).

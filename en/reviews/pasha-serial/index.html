Pasha: A Handcrafted Island of Calm in a World of Adrenaline Content

In short

“Pasha” is a 2026 series directed by Anton Kolomeets, Evgeniya Tamakhina. Every supporting character is genuinely distinctive and interesting; they are all, to borrow a line from children’s prose, “alive and glowing”.

BIBetsy Isakova·20 June 2026·updated 4 August 2026

About the series

Title
Pasha · Паша
Director
Anton Kolomeets, Evgeniya Tamakhina
Year
2026

20 June 2026

Once again a team in merch (I want some!!): Amediateka (a Russian streaming service) presents the series Pasha from my list of things to look forward to.

Judging by the first episode, this is a very touching, sparkling dramedy with gentle humour and intonation. Pasha, a Muscovite who is half Uzbek by ethnicity (and therefore runs into every stereotype), is trying to find somewhere to live for 10 days, because his mother’s friend has settled into his own flat with a child who has chickenpox (and Pasha, as luck would have it, never had it). So the hero literally and figuratively cannot find a home in his native city.

As the creators said, Askar Ilyasov is present in every scene of the series. And I will add that he sets the tone and mood of everything that happens, and that creates something inimitable. The premiere is on 10 July, and I cannot wait.

14 July 2026

I carried on watching Pasha after the first episode at Pilot (the Russian series festival in Ivanovo), and got exactly what I expected.

The series has an intonation rare these days, that of a kind of lyrical comedy where nothing especially funny seems to happen, sometimes even the opposite, something sad, and yet every so often you smile. Simply because life goes on, the sun peeks through the clouds, the neighbours act up and the relatives are toxic. Life does not end there, after all.

I will also keep watching closely because Askar Ilyasov promised something special in the seventh episode. Given that every episode of Pasha looks like a handcrafted island of calm in a world of adrenaline content, I am inclined to trust that advice.

4 August 2026

I watched all the episodes of Pasha in one go and had the loveliest evening.

Askar Ilyasov promised that something special awaited us in episode 7, and he did not deceive us: it is the peak of the season in conviction, sincerity and the absence of any sense of a boundary between the two sides of the screen. But personally I was most moved by episode 6, whether it was the images of a Moscow school in the 2000s that touched a nerve, or the rare chance that materialised before little Pasha to replay a dysfunctional scenario, this time with a happy outcome (in the best traditions of contemporary psychology).

In any case, I highly recommend watching Pasha: the value lies not only in the image of the protagonist but in the scattering of types surrounding him. Every supporting character is genuinely distinctive and interesting; they are all, to borrow a line from children’s prose, “alive and glowing”. A rare feeling for contemporary series.

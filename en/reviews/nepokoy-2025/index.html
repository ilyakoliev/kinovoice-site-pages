Restlessness: The Heroes Find Themselves in Something Like a Quantum Purgatory

In short

“Restlessness” is a 2025 film directed by Leonid Shmelkov. Yasha and Bernard are driving together to an unnamed distant destination and decide to spend the night on the way at an ordinary, moderately shabby roadside hotel called the Rosa.

20 July 2026

A great rarity: behind this stylish poster hides a no less intricately composed film, Restlessness (Nepokoy).

The plot of this adult animation is unpretentious precisely because quite early on it dissolves in favour of a controlled chaos of events, whose interpretation is left to the viewer’s discretion.

Yasha and Bernard are driving together to an unnamed distant destination and decide to spend the night on the way at an ordinary, moderately shabby roadside hotel called the Rosa. And there everything turns out to be stranger than strange.

Perhaps, without going into the details of what happens, it would be fair to say that the heroes have found themselves in something like a quantum purgatory. The hotel has a Madame, who is receptionist, cook, chambermaid and entertainer in one: she makes breakfasts from the dishes of roughly every cuisine in the world, hosts concerts, film clubs and all sorts of garden games. Only none of this gives the new guests any pleasure, because leaving the hotel proves impossible, first physically and then mentally.

What the hotel will be for you, and what the numerous metaphorical turns of the plot will turn into, is a matter of associations, imagination, viewing experience (if you wish, you will see Lynchian absurdity, and if you wish, a Wes Anderson aesthetic) and of the desire to reflect: Restlessness gives you full scope for that. For myself, I formulated what I saw as an extended visualisation of the opposite of what Pushkin wrote in the lines “there is no happiness in the world, but there is peace and freedom”. When there is everything for happiness but no possibility of making an independent decision in its favour, restlessness begins.

And this specific Restlessness can be experienced in cinemas (and only in cinemas; no release on streaming platforms has been announced) from 30 July.

7 August 2026

After this interview I wanted to rewatch Restlessness.

Director Leonid Shmelkov talks about the concept of this feature-length animation in such a way that you literally discover some things anew. It was pleasant to find that I had defined the essence of the pink hares and the annoying runners who flash on screen more than once exactly as was intended.

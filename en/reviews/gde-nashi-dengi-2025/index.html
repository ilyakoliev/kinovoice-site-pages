Where’s Our Money?: A Gangster Showdown in the Spirit of Guy Ritchie and Tarantino

In short

“Where’s Our Money?” is a 2024 film directed by Vladimir Zinkevich. This is a very brisk, dynamic and simply great gangster showdown in the spirit of Guy Ritchie and Quentin Tarantino, but with allowance made for our context.

BIBetsy Isakova·10 April 2025·updated 11 April 2025

About the film

Title
Where’s Our Money? · Где наши деньги?
Director
Vladimir Zinkevich
Year
2024

10 April 2025

I am admiring the stylish character posters for Where’s Our Money? (Gde nashi dengi?): from today this luxury can be seen in cinemas!

This is a very brisk, dynamic and simply great gangster showdown in the spirit of Guy Ritchie and Quentin Tarantino, but with allowance made for our context. Despite its modest running time, the film is built very intriguingly in terms of plot and characters, so your attention will be constantly captured by the motley inhabitants of the gangster landscape, each with their own quirk. The humour is top-notch too: it rides on the actors’ charisma, it is purely physical, and there are simply charming absurdities of various kinds. And concentrate when things head towards the finale: everything there is twisted beautifully)

In short, I warmly recommend the new release, and I will definitely rewatch it myself.

11 April 2025

By the way, Where’s Our Money? is notable for a couple more things.

First, I found a sweet reference there to Kuryer (“The Courier”), a film that, as it were, prefigures the gangster era. Like Ivan in The Courier, Kuzma Kotrelev’s character, nicknamed Nauka (“Science”), asks his friends whether they have a dream. The answers drown in the commotion of the plot, but what matters is the question itself, reminding us, as it were, that one should dream “of something great”. Especially in the context of a film focused on profit.

Second, the speech of the characters in Where’s Our Money? is real linguistic entertainment. “DelyugA”, with the stress on the “a”, “chikh-pykh” and other euphemisms are very interesting to decode or even to work into your own vocabulary (I confess, I am guilty, I love all these little words myself). And today I was also delighted to discover a glossary of this slang from director Vladimir Zinkevich and colleagues at Kinopoisk: the perfect cultural plan for a Friday evening.

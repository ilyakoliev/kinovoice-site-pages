Pluribus: The End of the World as a Chance to Amount to Something in Your Own Eyes

In short

“Pluribus” is a 2025 series directed by Gordon Smith, Vince Gilligan. It’s a vivid story that stirs up complicated emotions and questions about what you would do in the heroine’s place.

9 November 2025

I’ve watched the first episodes of Pluribus, the sci-fi drama from Vince Gilligan (Breaking Bad, Better Call Saul) about a virus that merges the minds of all the surviving humans into a single whole. All, that is, except the writer Carol — the heroine, played by Rhea Seehorn — and a dozen or so other earthlings from various countries.

It’s a vivid story that stirs up complicated emotions and questions about what you would do in the heroine’s place. That is exactly why the flawless way it’s all shot takes a back seat, its finely calibrated technique never overshadowing the moral dilemma.

Two episodes in, I’m still mulling it over. The analogies come thick and fast, from Covid and utopian hopes for ChatGPT to video-game NPCs and the trend for non-toxic communication. That is exactly what makes the story multi-layered and ambiguous — above all thanks to its unpredictability, which is quite rare and, of course, makes you want to keep watching. What I find especially interesting is how the heroine’s desire to✨save the world✨ (in the tradition of American cinema) actually serves as compensation for her hang-ups over being unable to write a book that meets her own high standards. The end of the world becomes a chance to amount to at least something in her own eyes — and for that, of course, you might well rise up against the whole world.

It’s curious how Vince Gilligan, known above all for the story of a man who falls ill and starts a new life, has arrived at the story of a woman around whom the whole world has fallen ill — and who has no plans whatsoever to start a new life :)

15 November 2025

Episode 3 of Pluribus

This is the first episode not written by Vince Gilligan, and I was prepared for the earlier pace to be gone. But not to this extent :) Then again, let’s remember that Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul were also pretty slow to build momentum on their way to greatness.

The series, like the members of the unified mind in its story, seems to have switched into a mode of saving energy and reallocating resources for some future, as yet not entirely clear, purposes.

Nor, by the way, is it obvious where the story might go from here. Everything seems to have stalled, and Carol isn’t asking any of the questions that we viewers would dearly love to have answered.

On the other hand, I’ve started to enjoy the slightly absurd comedy we’re being served. Showing the hundreds of people it takes to run a supermarket where the “self-sufficient and independent” heroine will buy everything for herself is funny and, what’s more, very sobering for everyone on this side of the screen about how everyday life actually works.

22 November 2025

Episode 4 of Pluribus flies by in an instant (and reminds you just how nice it is to binge Vince Gilligan’s shows).

It looks as though the first 3 episodes have taken the measure of the cruelty the new collective world is prepared to resort to. Now it’s Carol’s turn, and she doesn’t seem inclined to make allowances — neither for herself nor for the members of the new race. The unpredictability of her decisions, and of the surrounding world’s reactions to them, is still up to scratch, and that’s a pleasure to watch. Especially given the very easy-to-spot humour at the expense of AI chatbots, famous for their neutral, meaningless answers :)

I hope the second half of the season will give us a better idea of the goals of the heroine and of the “uninfected” like her. But it’s already clear that there’s no getting by without a second and even a third season.

28 November 2025

Episode 5 of Pluribus can, on the whole, almost be squeezed into a quote from A Clockwork Orange: “to have a glass of milk and do a bit of the old ultraviolence” (only, naturally, with a “not” in it).

It does the story a great deal of good to step beyond the aftermath of an apocalypse that has already happened and move into uncharted territory. The second half of the season is just the time to do it. In particular, Carol’s investigation into the love of milk among the members of the collective mind looks set to spill over (pun not intended) into one of the season’s main plot twists, one that will set future events in motion.

Incidentally, I don’t know whether it’s by design or simply arises as things go along, but the heroine’s attempts to lead the ranks of the scattered “survivors” are played quite comically (and she herself, for one, is aware of it). There’s no point waiting for a turning point at which the series steps into comedy for good or, on the contrary, drops all irony about the absurdity going on. As Better Call Saul and Breaking Bad taught us, Vince Gilligan is superb at combining all of this (I, at any rate, laughed without holding back at the bit with the drone that couldn’t carry off a bag of rubbish).

16 December 2025

Episode 7 of Pluribus (the most uneventful one, at first glance) could have been labelled “sleeping-pill” content.

Were it not for a couple of genuinely tense moments that show which of the characters is ready for what in this new world! If I ever have to explain what the term “slow burner” means, this is probably the episode I’ll point to. Vince Gilligan is brilliant at this — we remember it from the first season of Better Call Saul, which was slow from start to finish.

Here, too, nothing happens, and yet in the background there are shifts on the scale of tectonic plates as far as plot and character development go. Through Carol and Manousos, stagnation and backsliding are set against movement and self-overcoming. The difference in their motives becomes obvious at once: who really cared about saving the world, and who cared about being needed by someone in this new world. And from here on, clearly, the most interesting thing will be to see what endings characters with starting points like these arrive at.

23 December 2025

So what’s going on with Pluribus?

It’s unexpected (though understandable from the point of view of the show’s makers) that the meeting between Manousos and Carol is being put off for so long. Were it not for a couple of striking tendencies in these characters’ behaviour, episode 8 could even be called filler, given how utterly self-contained the two characters’ storylines are.

But what clearly awaits us is an impressive end to the season, with a meeting that ought to justify having to wait for the continuation. In the second season, the clash between ways of adapting to the new life will probably go on and be even fiercer than Carol’s rebellion at the very start. It’s impressive how long the show’s writers have managed to keep the new world order ambiguous — yet another successful example of grey morality, with arguments both for the infected and against them.

27 December 2025

The finale of Pluribus is as imposing as an atomic bomb.

It’s one of those episodes that made all the season’s slow and uneventful ones worth watching. In a modest 50 minutes the finale turns everything upside down twice, and for sheer density of moral about-faces it is simply unsurpassed.

Once again it turns out that man is an alien to man, even when the world is ending. And where the line runs that wipes out cultural contrasts, we find out only in the final shots.

Episode 9 of Pluribus has the second-highest IMDb rating of the season — 8.5 (against 9 for the pilot) — and that’s not a bad sign. I’ll be waiting for season two with moderate impatience :)

Pluribus: The End of the World as a Chance to Achieve at Least Something in Your Own Eyes

In short

“Pluribus” is a 2025 series directed by Gordon Smith, Vince Gilligan. This is a vivid story that provokes complex emotions and questions about what you would do in the heroine’s place.

BIBetsy Isakova·9 November 2025·updated 27 December 2025

About the series

Title
Pluribus
Director
Gordon Smith, Vince Gilligan
Year
2025

9 November 2025

I have watched the first episodes of Pluribus, the science-fiction drama from Vince Gilligan (Breaking Bad, Better Call Saul) about a virus that turns the minds of all surviving people into a single whole. Except for the writer Carol, the heroine played by Rhea Seehorn, and a dozen or so other earthlings from different countries.

This is a vivid story that provokes complex emotions and questions about what you would do in the heroine’s place. That is precisely why the impeccable way it is all shot recedes into the background, its calibrated technique not eclipsing the moral dilemma.

After two episodes I am still mulling it over. Analogies come flooding in, from Covid and utopian hopes for ChatGPT to NPCs from video games and the trend for eco-friendly communication. This is exactly what makes the story many-layered and ambiguous, first of all thanks to its unpredictability, which is quite rare and, of course, motivates you to keep watching. It is separately interesting how the heroine’s desire to✨save the world✨ (in the traditions of American cinema) actually serves to compensate for her complexes about her inability to create a book that meets her own high standards. The end of the world becomes a chance to achieve at least something in her own eyes, and for that, of course, one can rise up against the entire world.

It is curious how Vince Gilligan, known first and foremost for the story of a man who, having fallen ill, begins a new life, has arrived at the story of a woman around whom the whole world has fallen ill, and who has no plans whatsoever to begin a new life)

15 November 2025

Episode 3 of Pluribus

This is the first of the episodes whose script was not written by Vince Gilligan, and I was prepared for the previous pace to be gone. But not to that extent) Although, let us remember, Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul also wound up on their way to greatness quite slowly.

It seems that the series, like the representatives of the collective mind in the plot, has gone into energy-saving mode and the redistribution of resources for some future, as yet not entirely clear, goals.

Nor, incidentally, are the possible ways of continuing the story obvious. It is as if everything has stalled, and Carol asks none of the questions whose answers we viewers would very much like to know.

On the other hand, I have begun to enjoy the slightly absurd comedy we are being served. Showing the hundreds of people needed to run a supermarket where the “self-reliant and independent” heroine will buy everything for herself is funny and, moreover, very sobering for everyone on this side of the screen as to how everyday life is actually arranged.

22 November 2025

Episode 4 of Pluribus flies by in an instant (and reminds you how pleasant it is to binge Vince Gilligan’s series).

It seems that the measure of cruelty the new collective world is prepared to go to was taken in the first 3 episodes. Now it is Carol’s turn, and she, it seems, has no intention of making allowances, either for herself or for the representatives of the new race. The unpredictability of her decisions and of the surrounding world’s reactions to them remains at the same level, and that is pleasant to watch. Especially considering the very legible humour at the expense of neural networks, famous for their neutral, meaningless answers)

I hope the second half of the season will give more understanding of the goals of the heroine and of the “uninfected” like her. But it is already clear that a second and even a third season are unavoidable.

28 November 2025

Episode 5 of Pluribus on the whole almost fits into a quote from A Clockwork Orange: “to have a glass of milk and do a bit of the old ultraviolence” (only, naturally, with a “not”).

The story greatly benefits from going beyond the consequences of the apocalypse that has already happened and moving into unexplored territory. The second half of the season is exactly the time to do it. In particular, Carol’s investigation into the collective mind’s representatives’ love of milk seems set to spill over (pun not intended) into one of the season’s main plot twists, which will give impetus to future events.

Incidentally, I do not know whether it is intended or arises situationally, but the heroine’s attempts to lead the ranks of the scattered “survivors” are shown rather comically (and she reflects on this herself, too). There is no point waiting for a turning point when the series finally steps towards comedy or, on the contrary, loses all irony about the absurdity going on. As Better Call Saul and Breaking Bad taught us, Vince Gilligan combines all this superbly (I, at any rate, laughed without restraint at the moment with the drone that could not carry off the bag of rubbish).

16 December 2025

Episode 7 (the most uneventful at first glance) of Pluribus could be labelled “sleeping-pill” content.

Were it not for a couple of genuinely sharp moments that demonstrate which of the heroes is prepared for what in this new world! If I ever have to explain the meaning of the term “slow burner”, I will probably refer to precisely this episode. Vince Gilligan is magnificent at this, as we remember from the first season of Better Call Saul, which was slow from beginning to end.

Here too nothing happens, yet in the background there are shifts of tectonic plates by the standards of plot and character development. The example of Carol and Manousos contrasts stagnation and regression with movement and overcoming oneself. The difference in motivations immediately becomes obvious: for whom it really was important to save the world, and for whom to become needed by someone in this new world. And from here on, obviously, the most interesting thing is to follow what endings the characters with such starting points will arrive at.

23 December 2025

What about Pluribus?

It is unexpected (though understandable from the point of view of the series’ creators) that the meeting between Manousos and Carol is being put off for so long. Were it not for a couple of striking tendencies in these characters’ behaviour, episode 8 could even be called filler, for the exceptional self-containment of the two characters’ storylines.

But what obviously awaits us is an impressive season resolution with a meeting that should justify the need to wait for the continuation. Probably in the second season the struggle between methods of adapting to the new life will continue and will be even fiercer than Carol’s revolt at the very beginning. It is impressive how long the series’ authors manage to sustain the ambiguity of the new world order: another successful example of grey morality that contains arguments both for the infected and against them.

27 December 2025

The finale of Pluribus is as imposing as an atomic bomb.

This is one of those episodes for which all the slow and uneventful episodes of the season were worth watching. In the finale, in a modest 50 minutes, everything is turned upside down twice, and in the density of its moral flip-flops it is simply an unsurpassed level.

Once again it turns out that man is an alien to man, even under the conditions of the end of the world. And where the line lies that cancels out cultural contrasts, we learn only in the last frames.

Episode 9 of Pluribus has the second-highest rating of the season on IMDb, 8.5 (against 9 for the pilot), and that is a good sign. I will await the second season with moderate impatience)

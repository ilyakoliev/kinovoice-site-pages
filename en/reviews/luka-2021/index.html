Luca: Incredible Beauty, Both in Image and in Meaning

In short

“Luca” is a 2021 film directed by Enrico Casarosa. The film is very underrated. Incredible beauty, both in image and in meaning.

BIBetsy Isakova·9 June 2026

About the film

Title
Luca
Director
Enrico Casarosa
Year
2021

Silenzio Bruno!

The part of my brain responsible for the calendar arrival of the holidays ordered me to rewatch Pixar’s Luca. I have things to share:

  • The film is very underrated. Incredible beauty, both in image and in meaning. It was worth rewatching if only for the thought: people capable of accepting us even as sea monsters (read: as ourselves) do exist!

  • Actually, after the first viewing I did not remember that Luca himself is also a sea monster. I was sure he had merely made friends with a sea monster)) The Mandela effect has never been so personal.

  • Respect for how the film shows boys growing up: through friendship, vulnerability, curiosity and the right to be strange. Luca and Alberto grow up not because they become tougher, but because they learn to hear themselves and each other. For a healthy coming-of-age, it is just the thing.

  • This is one of the few Pixar works that do not flaunt their “adult side”, like, for example, both Inside Out films or Soul (with all due respect). Here, on the contrary, they play a game with the adult viewer to the very end called “interpret it however you like”. This is not unambiguous depression or the afterlife; here, go and work out what it even means to be a sea monster?

  • I have poured on the honey; now time not to forget the minus. The finale, to my taste, is made mostly in favour of pedagogical considerations (and therefore boring and blurred). One can argue about what the freedom the heroes strove for so hard actually is, and what their friendship meant to each of them. But in terms of meaning the film rather loses out from such a “something for everyone” decision. It is good that the picture’s merits outweigh this with room to spare.

A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms: The Sweetest Piece of the Game of Thrones Universe

In short

“A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms” is a 2026 series directed by Owen Harris, Sarah Adina Smith, Andrea Harkin. Dunk (Peter Claffey) is one of the most unaffected heroes I have seen in contemporary series.

BIBetsy Isakova·21 January 2026·updated 24 February 2026

About the series

Title
A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms
Director
Owen Harris, Sarah Adina Smith, Andrea Harkin
Year
2026

21 January 2026

The sweetest piece of the Game of Thrones universe has been found!

Such are my impressions after the first episode of A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms. In the course of one episode this story both charms and marks out its genre and tonal boundaries from the main franchise: everything here is somehow brighter, more naive, yet at the same time more accessible. Convincing and without unnecessary drama, as they say.

Dunk, a hedge knight (meaning he does not serve one lord but hires himself out to different ones, a kind of medieval freelancer), starts his path in the world of knights from scratch after the death of the old man he served as squire. At the very first inn he meets his future squire, Egg, a very stubborn but devoted boy. From that moment on, the proceedings steer into a charming buddy movie that holds your attention through two opposite extremes. All of it, incidentally, takes place 100 years before Daenerys’s fight for the throne, so Targaryens will turn up somewhere too.

Dunk (Peter Claffey) is one of the most unaffected heroes I have seen in contemporary series. His character works really well for trust in what is happening, and at the same time for lowering the pomposity, and that is a real find for Martin’s universe, where until now practically everything happened with a very straight face. And here it is the irony that wins you over most of all: it turns out to be appropriate even in the cruel Middle Ages)

29 January 2026

And A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms continues to delight.

The second episode altogether creates the impression of a great family dramedy in a fantasy setting (well, if you forget the first scene). Between Duncan and Egg a very cool, complementary bond has taken shape, in which the child is sometimes wiser than the adult, and the adult more unaffected than the child. All this creates a great viewing experience: it seems to be made for adults, yet with a warmth and humour that we often get only in the children’s content segment.

But although there is none of the stuffy arrogance of the other parts of the Game of Thrones universe, the pace is still quite slow. 2 of the season’s 6 episodes have passed, and our protagonist still has not started taking part in the tournament. They take a long time harnessing the horses, but after House of the Dragon we are used to it.

3 February 2026

The finale of the third episode of A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms is greatness in its purest form!

One scene pays off both the slow pace of the first episodes and the apparent toothlessness of what was happening. The plot twist revealing Egg’s origins also lands perfectly in the middle of the season: 3 episodes still lie ahead, and that is exactly the right amount of time to reveal the character in a new capacity.

Separately I want to praise the showrunners for the slow build-up within this particular episode: from an absolute buddy movie there was a smooth and hypnotic transition to palace intrigue. And here, as they say, watch the hands: in the final minute of the episode every glance, shift of intonation and gesture matters. I rewatched this scene about 4 times and, of course, am now eagerly awaiting the continuation (and I am also sorely tempted to run a couple of chapters ahead of the series in the book)

7 February 2026

Thanks to the Super Bowl, HBO decided to release the new episode of A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms a couple of days early (so that the game would not steal the viewers’ attention))

As for the content of the episode, it is simply a raw nerve 35 minutes long. The protagonist’s nightmare (in the literal and figurative senses), where absolutely unsolvable problems arise one after another on the snowball principle (true to life, on the whole).

But while watching, of course, you admire the staging of this escalating plot 1000%. Peter Claffey’s acting range is revealed brilliantly: we have seen him embarrassed, cheerful, upset, and now this palette has gained the shade of a genuinely wounded knight who no longer resembles the awkward peasant of the previous episodes. Before us is no longer a boy but a man, as the saying goes. And watching him is a great stroke of viewer’s luck.

18 February 2026

One of the episodes of A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms, by the fine tradition of HBO projects, was bound to lead us away from the main plot, introducing us to the hero’s past or to entirely new characters.

That episode turned out to be the fifth, combining both Duncan’s biography and a wonderful battle scene. The device that allowed these events to run in parallel is in some ways already familiar: recall how in Game of Thrones, at the Battle of the Blackwater, Tyrion fell down and missed everything. Only there it looked more like budget-saving, whereas in A Knight it is a clever way of handling the modest running time of an episode and the viewer’s attention.

Nevertheless, never before have I wanted so much for a season finale to run a couple of hours)

24 February 2026

The finale of A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms leaves a feeling of complete harmony from what you have watched.

The six episodes of the first season are a wonderful example of the combination of buddy movie, gripping action, drama in measured doses and calibrated humour. This leaves the viewer (no matter how much they are into the lore of the Game of Thrones universe) practically no chance of getting bored or, God forbid, leaving dissatisfied. An exemplary miniseries that will obviously take its place among the best of 2026.

It is especially heartening that the series has already been renewed for a second season, which will come out in 2027. Add it to your watchlists and finish reading Martin’s source text)

Daring: Every Chance of Standing Alongside Brigada

In short

“Daring” is a 2024 series directed by Yuri Bykov. The story is very well constructed, made up of two stories developing in parallel, in the present day and in the 1990s.

BIBetsy Isakova·24 October 2024·updated 18 September 2025

About the series

Title
Daring · Лихие
Director
Yuri Bykov
Year
2024

24 October 2024

The series Daring (Likhie, “The Dashing Ones”, a nod to the “dashing 90s”) has every chance of standing alongside Brigada (the cult 2002 gangster series) as a saga of equally epochal sweep about crime and the people who are drawn into it (not) of their own will.

Daring, as always with Yuri Bykov, sets out the characters’ initial motivation very intelligibly. Artyom Bystrov’s character, Pavel, a simple taiga gamekeeper who makes a living from honey, finds that in 1991 there is nothing to live on, while at the same time his former fellow villager is buying a whole flat in Khabarovsk. A thirst not even for a beautiful life but simply for one that is not humiliating takes hold of the hero, and from then on he is ready to take the word of the fellow villager promising the same “well-paid job”. Naturally, such a job implies nothing good.

The story is very well constructed, made up of two stories developing in parallel, in the present day and in the 1990s. On the one hand, we do not even have to wait 16 episodes to understand where, 30 years later, our protagonist’s son (his main accomplice from the first episodes) has ended up. On the other, the inserts of the present let us not simply watch silently, yet again, the deeds of bygone days in the 1990s, but see the delayed consequences of that time, which now seem invisible. A series I will be watching closely to the end.

18 September 2025

The opposition of father and son Likhovtsev in the second episode of the continuation of Daring is constructed as convincingly as possible.

Since the start of the season, only the very worst has been coming out in Zhenka, even if with good intentions (the “help” with his younger brother’s screw-up, for example). The character turns out to be somehow rotten, insincere, two-faced, even by the standards of those around him. Either the psyche of a child immersed in bloody business from childhood could not take it, or he simply absorbed the worst of the atmosphere of his time, but the result is plain to see. I admire Egor Kenzhametov in this role. A curious coincidence that he also played the younger brother of Yura Borisov’s character in the film Byk (“The Bull”): in that role Egor, on the contrary, had to stay clear of the harshness of character, while here he goes all out.

In contrast to him, the father, Pavel Likhovtsev, oddly enough, seems brighter. It is astonishing how, with so much blood on his hands, the character managed to preserve (or at any rate to be able to remember) a sincere, bright love for his wife and some sort of value system. Artyom Bystrov’s performance in this role was wonderful from the start, but now it acquires some new importance.

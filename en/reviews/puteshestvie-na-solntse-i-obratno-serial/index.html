A Trip to the Sun and Back: The Terrifying 1990s You Take for a Fairy Tale

In short

“A Trip to the Sun and Back” is a 2025 series directed by Roman Mikhailov. Ruslan, the fairy-tale-ethereal protagonist played by Oleg Chugunov, was truly born lucky.

BIBetsy Isakova·25 July 2025·updated 13 August 2025

About the series

Title
A Trip to the Sun and Back · Путешествие на солнце и обратно
Director
Roman Mikhailov
Year
2025

25 July 2025

Having watched 4 episodes of A Trip to the Sun and Back, (Puteshestvie na solntse i obratno) in a row, I not only immersed myself in the atmosphere of the genuinely terrifying 1990s (which, though we are tired of them, are as good as can be here), taking it for a fairy tale, but also made 2 interesting observations:

1️⃣ Ruslan, the fairy-tale-ethereal protagonist played by Oleg Chugunov, was truly born lucky. Over the first episodes we see how in the 1990s his crew of 4 lads seems to dissolve into thin air. One was killed in prison, the second became a junkie, and what will happen to the third is not yet clear. Ruslan, as if at crossroads marked by stones with inscriptions like “go left and you will find death”, very successfully fits into the right turns and comes out of the water dry, out of the fire alive, and so on. By the standards of the era’s setting, such luck is easily taken for a fairy-tale miracle.

2️⃣ I do not know why (all right, I do know, but I will tell you in a separate post), but in atmosphere the series reminds me of the credits of the project Deti peremen. (“Children of Change”) The same hypnotic feeling of watching a vanishing world over which the foot of cruel reality is already raised, ready to trample it all. Only, if in the credits it is a combination of shots like a family chronicle, in the series it is impulsive attempts to live a young and vivid life in a stale vacuum unfit for anything living at all. Terrifying and impossibly recognisable.

30 July 2025

The fifth episode of A Trip to the Sun and Back, as happens with the best episodes of series, simply demands a checklist of the beautiful things seen:

  • Ruslan’s trip to St Petersburg, utterly Balabanovian and in the best traditions of Brother, where, as usual, all the strength lies. Except that he introduced himself as a student rather than a clerk. Except that they did not issue him Danila Bagrov’s sweater.

  • A card-game scene in the spirit either of Guy Ritchie or of the forthcoming project Poker for Us. A wonderful attraction of reality in the middle of a fairy-tale narrative.

  • The inimitable feeling of fully understanding the emotions of Vitya, the young crime boss played by Ivan Ivashov, who delivered an impressive monologue about a broken future and the forced repetition of his parents’ fate on the ruins of his hopes. For me he is the real protagonist of the episode.

It seems there is more and more of the second element than the first in the series’ magical realism, and watching this is unsettling.

6 August 2025

The sixth episode of A Trip to the Sun and Back is the penultimate one, slowing the pace a little in order to show more expressively what happens when the past inside your head does not get on with the present outside it.

The madness quietly spreading across the screen seems to hint that Ruslan has now arrived at a real crossroads rather than a fairy-tale one ➖ Either choose his own time, unsettled and shelterless as it is, or remain forever in the bright memory of childhood with a bride from fairy-tale books, like the boy Bananan “in the sheltered world of dreams” (from the film Assa), and, as Shpalikov wrote, “where mother is young and father is alive”. Here the father, though physically alive, is not quite alive in spirit, as if a “former person” of a departed era. The same, however, can be said of the mother, who has lost her bearings in the new world. Everyone has lost them, really: just consider the rehearsal of funeral speeches at the New Year table.

If earlier the finale seemed to promise a bright resolution against all odds, now it rather frightens: if only Ruslan’s choice turns out not to be ruinous.

13 August 2025

A Trip to the Sun and Back has ended.

The last episode has, perhaps, absorbed the whole spectrum of emotions the characters lived through over the season. It was frightening, eerie, hopeless, inescapable, bright and sad. The finale keeps you in suspense better than any thriller: thanks to the blurring of genre, it is genuinely impossible to predict what exactly will happen and how irreparable it will be. It is a great piece of work to make the resolution gripping without losing the harmony set by the previous episodes. Such an ending makes you feel sincere gratitude to the authors: the characters deserved nothing else.

Of course, because of the plot twist with Laszlo, who came in handy in the criminal schemes, the thought of Balabanov’s Danila will not leave you. Also, it seems, a bit of a holy fool, but he found his place. St Petersburg belongs there too.

And the scene in the train vestibule both horrifies and delights. After everything he has been through, Ruslan could have perished there or been born anew. How good that it was the latter.

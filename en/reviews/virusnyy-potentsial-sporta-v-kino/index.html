The Viral Potential of Sport in Cinema and the Cannibalisation of Attention

BIBetsy Isakova·27 January 2026

On the viral potential of sport in cinema and the cannibalisation of attention

I cannot help comparing Heated Rivalry with Marty Supreme in terms of viral potential through the display of sport. In both, the focus is on athletes, they attend international competitions, and this gives rise to many spectacular scenes of actual sporting contests. Both works have a kind of public resonance: the hockey story created a fully fledged new fandom, while the plot about a ping-pong player is already shaping Oscar expectations for Timothée Chalamet.

And yet in the case of Heated Rivalry we see a universal craze for hockey: growth in ticket sales for hockey games (Bloomberg writes of growth of more than 20%), growth in sign-ups for hockey clubs and other external effects noticeable from online trends. With Marty Supreme, though, there is practically no such effect, even though ping-pong was aestheticised there as much as possible (just recall the film’s orange promo in honour of the hero’s branded balls). There is, of course, growth in ticket sales for ping-pong matches, also by more than 20%, as the Guardian claims, but this is completely baffling on intuitive assumptions.

Of course, the works are of fundamentally different genres, never mind that one is a series and the other a film. But it seems to me that had Marty not come out exactly at the peak of the whole world finishing Heated Rivalry, our feeds would have been filled with the aesthetics of table tennis rather than hockey)

Pilot 2026: Independent Projects I Want to See in Full

In short

As the team behind the project put it, “we talk about traditional values in a non-traditional way”.

Betsy Isakova·17 June 2026·updated 22 June 2026

17 June 2026

I’m plunging into the film-festival atmosphere: Pilot (the Russian series festival in Ivanovo) is starting, which means that over the next 5 days this channel will fill up with reviews of the first episodes of all kinds of series.

I went through the (very hefty) programme in advance and noted down the shows whose descriptions sound most interesting.

  • Vor v zagone («Вор в загоне», “A Thief in the Pen”, a play on vor v zakone, “thief in law”): a series with Denis Vlasenko (you remember my principles). A comedy about a psychologist who studies his fiancée’s relatives and assigns each of them a mental disorder of their own. I’m hoping for About This Very Thing with a psychological slant

  • Festival («Фестиваль»): a series about a film festival, and what could be better than watching something like that at a film festival?! The director is Alexey Kamynin, who previously made the wonderful and very funny film Russian Spleen («Хандра»). The cast of the new show includes Daniil Vorobyov, Mikhail Troynik and, incidentally, my colleague Artyom Remizov

  • Second Wind: a tragicomedy by Ruslan Bratov with Alexander Pal, about trying to find yourself in life and difficult relationships with the people closest to you. I trust Bratov to tell this story in a dramatically funny way.

  • Jive: a high-energy show about dancesport from the production company Sreda for Okko (the Russian streaming service), so I expect the quality to match. The cast includes David Sokratyan, Anastasia Ukolova, Svyatoslav Rogozhan and lots of cool young actors.

  • Living Anew: a comedy about a second chance to live your best years (literally and figuratively: the main character is transported from the present day to 2002). The cast includes Alexander Yatsenko, Elena Podkaminskaya and other actors you always believe. Shades of Comeback, judging by the era.

  • Pasha with Askar Ilyasov (I wrote about it here)

18 June 2026

The block of independent projects has been shown, and here are the ones I hope we will get to see in full in the future:

GBU “Zhizn” («ГБУ „Жизнь“», “State Institution ‘Life’”)

As the team behind the project put it, “we talk about traditional values in a non-traditional way”. The series will be about an unusual funeral home that tries to make even the part of life that has to do with death life-affirming. A gentle, blackish (naturally) but very likeable comedy.

Volshebnaya pyl («Волшебная пыль», “Magic Dust”)

A talented chemist (Yulia Volkova) loses her job and suddenly discovers that her neighbour is cooking up something suspicious. Having ruined the neighbour’s homemade-cosmetics business with her vigilance, the woman is plotting something.

Vverkh za mechtoy («Вверх за мечтой», “Up After the Dream”)

A romantic comedy with Polina Denisova and Grisha Vernik. An underrated producer finds herself forced to work with an arrogant showman (a former university classmate, to boot), but their hostility gradually gives way to passion. The “enemies to lovers” mechanism works brilliantly on screen, and I have known one of the producers, Liza Bulatova, since school, so I believe in this story.

Privet, Karolinka («Привет, Каролинка», “Hi, Karolinka”)

A children’s adventure fantasy about a girl who investigates her grandmother’s mysterious disappearance with the help of a magic piece of chalk. What sets the project apart is that the main villain is played by the stylist Alexander Rogov. A spiritual heir to Curious Varvara (the children’s detective series), which makes it a bid for a quality children’s series.

19 June 2026

The morning starts with independent projects: today it’s sci-fi, aliens and mockumentary.

Shisha

A project about provincial evil spirits who live in the age of social media and decide to relaunch their villainous brand to keep the “Agency of Dirty Tricks” from being shut down. For this, “Kolkhoz Production” is brought in. A little raw (which is perfectly normal for the pilot of an independent project), but already a very charming spiritual heir to What We Do in the Shadows and The Magic District (Volshebny uchastok).

Kosmos nash (“Space Is Ours”)

A blogger who films so-called contactees (people who have made contact with aliens) hits a million subscribers and suddenly loses his dog. What he gains instead is a suspicious fangirl with the habits of an alien. Another pilot that is likeable at the concept level and has every chance of success once it has been polished.

19 June 2026

Two decent TV shows: Angel («Ангел») from TV-3 and Ilya Murovets («Илья Муровец», a play on the epic hero Ilya Muromets and MUR, the Moscow criminal investigation department) from Channel 5 (both are Russian TV channels).

In Angel, Kuzma Saprykin plays the angel Serafim, sent down from heaven to watch over an investigator who has recently lost her husband. A nod of sorts to the film Moy paren angel («Мой парень ангел», “My Boyfriend Is an Angel”). It sticks in the memory for the perfect choice of actor for the angel, and also for comic moments teetering between cringe and wisdom (I noted down, for instance, the line “if someone has wiped their feet on you once, don’t go rolling yourself back up like a worn-out doormat” — the audience applauded).

Ilya Murovets is a police procedural with a fairy-tale accent. It’s all just as the Channel 5 people described it at the morning industry session: a fairy tale according to Propp, magical helpers and a happy ending. The only thing that lets it down is the none-too-polished AI-generated inserts of the hero’s flashbacks to his past.

20 June 2026

Today’s block of independent projects delighted me with its youth themes:

Kod 102 («Код 102», “Code 102”)

Possibly the strongest independent project of the year. A vibey, neon-lit series about young musicians from Ufa who are trying to break through and make their dream come true. The project has its distinctive features: the local colour of Bashkortostan and Ufa, the involvement of local performers, and an excellent cast: Chingiz Garaev, Arseny Kasperovich, Pyotr Skvortsov and other cool actors.

#Ne_moya_zhizn («#Не_моя_жизнь», “#Not_My_Life”)

A story about the destructive effect of social media on our lives, told through teenage characters. A high-school girl who dreams of becoming a blogger happens to find a phone and starts posting videos from it. After that everything starts going her way, but as soon as the owner demands the phone back, things stop being pleasant.

Tozhe mne lyubov («Тоже мне любовь», “Call That Love”)

A drama about different kinds of relationships: between romantic partners, between sisters, with yourself. All of them, naturally, unhealthy. It plays very much in the spirit of the recent Half Man.

21 June 2026

And two more projects that struck me as promising:

The Captain’s Daughter («Капитанская дочка»): a fresh screen adaptation of the classic with Oleg Savostyuk, Evgeny Tkachuk, Viktoria Isakova and other heavyweight actors. It sounds interesting and looks striking; what remains to be seen is how it will be pitched.

Kamenskaya. Reboot («Каменская. Перезагрузка»): a relaunch of the classic series (the 2000s detective show based on Alexandra Marinina’s novels) with Yulia Khlynina in the lead. She was excellent as the investigator in Overheard in Rybinsk, so I believe in this character too.

21 June 2026

All the winners of Pilot 2026

  • Best series pilot: Second Wind
  • Best series pilot (audience choice): Problem Child
  • Best director of a series pilot: Ruslan Bratov (Second Wind)
  • Best pilot screenplay: Anton Kolomeets and Evgeniya Tamakhina (Pasha)
  • Best ensemble cast: the series Festival («Фестиваль»)
  • Best actor in a series: Askar Ilyasov (Pasha)
  • Best actress in a series: Yulianna Mikhnevich (she appeared in three of the festival’s series)
  • Best documentary series: Virus popsy («Вирус попсы», “The Pop Virus”)
  • Best second season of a series: Old School
  • Most anticipated series: Krasnaya shambala («Красная шамбала», “Red Shambhala”)

22 June 2026

Pilot 2026 has wrapped up, and Olya and I have put together all the festival’s series for Kinopoisk (Russia’s largest film database, media and streaming service) in a single roundup.

This year’s programme is (to my taste) a strong one, and I have been sharing my most vivid impressions here throughout the festival. And in the piece, do pay attention to the award-winning series: they are an absolute must-watch on every count (Askar Ilyasov personally advised me to watch Pasha, which starts on 10 July, all the way to the end, so there is every reason to trust the tip).

22 June 2026

And I can’t help sharing this: I’ve come back from Pilot charged up for a whole year ahead with love of cinema, joy, and the energy to share my favourite series and support their premieres.

The strongest of programmes, the most caring organisation and a practically family atmosphere: every year I’m amazed at how good it is, and about an hour after the closing I start looking forward to next year. That, it seems to me, is unique and priceless.

I value beyond measure the chance to be part of something this lively, the trust of the organisers and your patience, dear readers (let’s be honest, a flood of posts like this doesn’t happen often). Thank you.

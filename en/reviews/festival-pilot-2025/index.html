Pilot 2025: Impressions of the Competition Pilots

In short

The festival opens with Constantinople — like last year’s opener, a textbook classical historical drama with its own strengths and weaknesses.

Betsy Isakova·31 May 2025·updated 25 June 2025

31 May 2025

Festival news!

My beloved series festival, Pilot, held in Ivanovo, where I’ll be from 19 to 22 June, has announced its opening series.

It will be Constantinople («Константинополь»; formerly titled Izgoi, “The Outcasts”), the story of the White émigré Neratov (Alexander Ustyugov), who lost pretty much everything in the Civil War and ended up in Constantinople. No longer as a prominent White Guard colonel, though, but as a rank-and-file refugee and, as we can guess, one with precious few rights. And it is the rights of Russian émigrés that he will be fighting for.

For the second year running, Pilot is opening with a large-scale historical drama. Last year it was the slightly hectic series Plevako, about the famous lawyer of the last pre-revolutionary years, and that’s an interesting coincidence.

Perhaps it is precisely the final years of the Russian Empire and the start of the revolution that are now among the most in-demand historical parallels cinema is making sense of. So I can’t wait to see it!

11 June 2025

And here is part of the programme of Pilot (the Russian series festival in Ivanovo), which is about to begin!

In this competition I’ll naturally be especially looking forward to the screenings of The Surgeon (Khirurg), with Sergei Gilev in the lead, and Not at Ease («Не в своей тарелке»), a sci-fi comedy with Yuri Stoyanov and Denis Prytkov.

16 June 2025

And, at last, the main competition of the Pilot festival! Time to pick the most anticipated premieres :)

Personally, I’m very curious about:

  • 16 let i 9 mesyatsev («16 лет и 9 месяцев», “16 Years and 9 Months”): a teen story starring Oleg Savostyuk, whom I’m always glad to see

  • Dear Willie («Дорогой Вилли»): Sergei Makovetsky as Brezhnev, come on!

  • Breathe (Dyshi): directed by Anna Kuznetsova, who made Intermission (Kanikuly), a very important and much-noticed film from last year

  • Jack Russell (Zheka Rassel): Eldar Kalimulin’s character turns into a Jack Russell terrier. I’m expecting a great comedy :)

  • Old School (Oldskul): this show about an old-school teacher has an atmospheric teaser that really whets the appetite

  • Popados («Попадос», “Busted”): Makar Khlebnikov and Timofey Tribuntsev at the centre of a crime story — a promising duo (especially going by the Tribuntsev and Minekaev pairing in Overheard in Rybinsk)

I’ll be writing about all of it from the festival.

19 June 2025

And over the next 4 days there will be lots of reviews on the channel of what I’ve watched at the Pilot festival (the Russian series festival in Ivanovo).

It’s my second festival, and I’d been really looking forward to the trip: it’s always a celebration of film and a chance to talk to all sorts of people from the industry.

This time 17 “pilots” are in competition: first episodes of series that will later (most of them) come out on our streaming services. Expect reviews (and here I’ve rounded up descriptions of the premieres I’m most looking forward to)! Last year, for instance, Pilot screened She’s So Cool and Give a Show!, both of which went on to be big hits.

The festival also has an industry programme and a documentary series competition — I’ll try to write about the most interesting news from there. And at the end the jury hands out awards in various categories: best pilot, naturally, best actor/actress and so on. Last year it was really interesting to compare my guesses with the jury’s decision, so I’m already looking forward to it.

19 June 2025

My colleagues gave it their blessing, so I’ll post a joke I came up with back when the Pilot jury was announced

Life is not the same without a Kott! (a play on the Russian saying “life is not the same without a cat”, kot being Russian for “cat”)

The joke is that the 2024 jury included the director Vladimir Kott, and this year’s jury has his brother Alexander! A must-have surname at the festival :)

19 June 2025

The festival (Pilot, the Russian series festival in Ivanovo) opens with Constantinople — like last year’s opener, a textbook classical historical drama with its own strengths and weaknesses. In the first episode, the adventures of refugees from the Russian Empire in Constantinople are more of a statement of intent, and the plot loses out to the artistry with which this world has been worked out.

We have an officer who has lost his whole family and his faith in military honour, a quick-witted doctor (the most interesting character so far, played by Kirill Käro) and a “businessman” desperate to get back the gold confiscated by Constantinople’s customs officers. It’s hard to tie these storylines together as yet, so that will be a job mostly for the genre’s most devoted fans.

20 June 2025

Is it hard to adapt texts for the screen?

Mikhail Kitaev is talking about it in Pilot’s industry section. You can see the arguments for and against on the slide, and on the whole, of course, it’s hard to disagree that these adaptations are a hugely exciting business and, at the same time, a fiendishly difficult one. After all, every reader already has their own picture in their head!

I especially liked how Kinopoisk (Russia’s largest film database, media and streaming service) played on the conflicting reviews of Crime and Punishment, the cornerstone of the heated debates of autumn 2024. Half the audience gave it extremely high ratings, and the other half gave it 1 out of 10. It ended up at 5.8 on Kinopoisk. The platform gathered all sorts of reviews and made a brilliant video inviting you to pick a side — a great piece of image-building! Speaking of Crime and Punishment, by the way: which side are you on? :)

20 June 2025

Full House is a story that is interesting first and foremost for being experimental. It mixes a little medical drama, a little of the supernatural, a little procedural and a little of Bulgakov’s A Young Doctor’s Notebook. Not everything runs smoothly, but if they keep working on it, as its makers said they would, it could potentially be a good thing.

The story of a principled medical resident in the far north makes for slightly uncomfortable viewing — just enough to convey how the heroine feels in a new place where her colleagues’ trust is at zero and all around her are bureaucracy and hard-drinking surgeons. Pyotr Fyodorov, incidentally, is very good as one of the latter. I’m curious to see how his clearly drawn conflict with the heroine, played by Maria Lisovaya, develops. For a clash of experience and idealism like that, it’s probably worth waiting for the remaining episodes.

20 June 2025

Remember the title Popados (“Busted”): it’s a brisk crime story with new faces and already seasoned actors. I’d been looking forward to it largely because of the pairing of Makar Khlebnikov and Timofey Tribuntsev — and they really are wonderful here, playing father and son.

The plot structure does a great job of combining flashbacks with the present, where the consequences of the teenagers’ prank have already set in. Flirting with the criminal world lands the characters in big trouble, which brings to mind Na avtomate (“On Autopilot”), a series that had substantial things to say on the same themes. At a good pace, the pilot gave every storyline a decent set-up, so you really do want to wait for more.

21 June 2025

With all my heart I hope the project Bukhanka Chyornogo (“Chyorny’s Loaf”, a pun on the UAZ “loaf” van) finds its platform and gets filmed to the end: it’s a real discovery among the independent projects.

The uproariously funny Lyokha — the protagonist, whom I privately dubbed “the plush gangster” — works as a waiter in his father’s coffee shop, hates hipsters with their almond-milk lattes and threatens them with a pistol when they complain about the food (on closer inspection it’s just a pistol-shaped lighter). A portrait of Sasha Bely (the gangster hero of Brigada) hangs on the mirror in his room, and he himself is grotesquely stuck in the 1990s, when his grandfather Grisha Chyorny was running things. In search of former glory he heads to his grandmother’s village in Karelia, where all that is left of his grandfather’s legacy is a UAZ van — that very Chyorny’s Loaf (my applause for the show’s inventive title). An attempt to get his grandfather’s Merc back ends in trouble, and after that all we can do is wait for a whole season :)

My Garazhniki (“Garage People”) of 2025.

21 June 2025

Sunny in a southern way and pleasantly naive, the series Winemakers neatly sets out a screenwriting device that has become a favourite of late: the working class colliding with arty bohemians.

This time the thing to be split is a winery. A trio of proletarians from Chelyabinsk arrive in Gelendzhik to claim the inheritance of a relative who dropped off the radar 20 years ago (and has now died altogether). The local owners (and the late relative’s business partners), played by Andrey Urgant and Elena Podkaminskaya, are in shock. Podkaminskaya, by the way, plays the winery’s manager — every bit as wonderfully as, many years ago, she played the manager of a French restaurant in The Kitchen (the hit sitcom).

The standard comic situations come with added wine snobbery and a mismatch of, shall we say, gastronomic cultures, which is made up for by a romantic match :) I hope the story opens up like a good wine.

21 June 2025

It’s great that so many of the shows at this Pilot are specifically about women and their problems.

Fake is a superb illustration of how utterly defenceless you are online and, following on from that, how completely exposed you are to people who don’t believe you and pass judgement. A true horror story, in which the monsters hide not under the bed but in the comments.

A starry-eyed biology teacher (Anastasia Krasovskaya, who for the second festival running has landed harrowing scenes of violence) runs into brutal online stalking: it all looks as though she is taking part in erotic chats under her own name. Her identity has literally been stolen. The young woman tries to find the culprit, and that gives the story a headlong pace and the right kind of tension. It’s frightening, and intriguing, to think how this story might end ⬅️

22 June 2025

The results of the Pilot 2025 competitions are about to be announced, but my colleagues and I pooled our opinions a little earlier in a special table by Komendant kinokreposti («Комендант кинокрепости», “The Commandant of the Film Fortress”, a Telegram channel).

I’m very grateful for this chance to compare notes with colleagues and get an objective assessment of what we’ve seen. And, of course, I’ll be watching for matches with the jury’s decisions.

22 June 2025

By the way, about the vote my colleagues and I held on Pilot’s main competition programme: I have a little visualisation and a few interesting facts about us!

It turns out that among us there is a kindest critic (yours truly), a strictest critic (@ershovfilm) and several people whose opinions are very close to one another :)

And opinions differed most over the series Full House: its spread of scores is the most noticeable! How cool that we have such different views and opinions, and that we’ve brought them together and put them on record.

25 June 2025

And last but not least, in the wake of Pilot!

Kinopoisk (Russia’s largest film database, media and streaming service) and I have gathered all the series from the festival into one piece, for which I wrote about 9 of the 18 series. I highly recommend it as a retrospective and as a watchlist for the future :) (by the way, for a bit of extra entertainment as you read, you can try to guess from the writing style exactly which of the series I covered myself)

I also had a small hand in the piece on the industry programme — it gave us not only the much-discussed portrait of the average Russian woman, but also some interesting news and a few anticipated projects. Short and to the point!

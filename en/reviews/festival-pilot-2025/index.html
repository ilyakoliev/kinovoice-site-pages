Pilot 2025: Impressions of the Competition Pilots

In short

The festival opens with Konstantinopol, which, as last year, is an exemplary classical historical drama with its own advantages and shortcomings.

BIBetsy Isakova·19 June 2025·updated 21 June 2025

19 June 2025

The festival (Pilot, the Russian series festival in Ivanovo) opens with Konstantinopol (“Constantinople”), which, as last year, is an exemplary classical historical drama with its own advantages and shortcomings. The adventures of refugees from the Russian Empire in Constantinople in the first episode are rather of a declarative nature and, in terms of plot, fall short of the artistic elaboration of this world.

Before us is an officer who has lost his whole family and his faith in military honour, a quick-witted doctor (so far the most interesting character, played by Kirill Käro) and a “merchant” thirsting to get back the gold confiscated by the Constantinople customs officers. Tying these lines together is difficult so far, so that will be a task mostly for the most devoted fans of the genre.

20 June 2025

Full House is a story interesting first and foremost for its experimental nature. It combines a little medical drama, a little mysticism, a little procedural, a little of Bulgakov’s A Young Doctor’s Notebook. Not everything is smooth, but if they polish it, as the creators said, it is potentially a good thing.

The plot about a principled medical resident in the far north looks somewhat uncomfortable, exactly enough to convey the heroine’s state in a new place where there is zero trust from colleagues and bureaucracy and drinking surgeons all around. Incidentally, Pyotr Fyodorov is rather good as the latter. It will be interesting to see how his clearly drawn conflict with the heroine, played by Maria Lisova, develops. For the sake of such a clash of experience and idealism, it is probably worth waiting for the remaining episodes.

20 June 2025

Remember the title Popados (“Busted”): it is a brisk piece of crime action with new faces and already established artists. I was looking forward to it largely because of the duo of Makar Khlebnikov and Timofey Tribuntsev, and they really are wonderful here, playing father and son.

The plot structure neatly combines flashbacks with a present in which the consequences of the teenagers’ prank have already arrived. Flirting with the criminal world turns into big problems for the heroes, and here one recalls Na avtomate (“On Autopilot”), a project that reflected substantively on the same themes. The pilot gave all its lines worthy set-ups at a good pace, so one really does want to wait for the continuation.

21 June 2025

With all my heart I wish the project Bukhanka Chyornogo (“Chyorny’s Loaf”, a pun on the UAZ “loaf” van) to find its platform and be filmed to the end: it is a real discovery among the independent projects.

The hysterically funny Lyokha, the protagonist, whom I privately dubbed “the plush gangster”, works as a waiter in his father’s coffee shop, hates hipsters with their almond-milk lattes and threatens them with a pistol over complaints about the dishes (on closer inspection it is just a pistol-shaped lighter). In his room a portrait of Sasha Bely (the gangster hero of Brigada) hangs on the mirror, and he himself is grotesquely stuck in the 1990s, when his grandfather Grisha Chyorny ran things. In search of past greatness he goes to his grandmother’s Karelian village, where the only thing left of his grandfather’s legacy is a UAZ van, that very Chyorny’s Loaf (my applause for the project’s resourceful title). An attempt to get back his grandfather’s Merc turns into problems, and from there all that remains is to wait for a whole season)

My Garazhniki (“Garage People”) of 2025.

21 June 2025

The southern-sunny and pleasantly naive series Vinodely (“The Winemakers”) succinctly declares a lately favourite screenwriting device: the collision of the working class with the aestheticising bohemia.

This time a winery is to be divided. A trio of proletarians from Chelyabinsk come to Gelendzhik for the inheritance of a relative who vanished from the radar 20 years ago (and has now died altogether). The local owners (and partners of the deceased relative), played by Andrey Urgant and Elena Podkaminskaya, are in shock. Podkaminskaya, by the way, plays the winery’s manager, no less beautifully than many years ago she played the manager of a French restaurant in Kukhnya (“The Kitchen”, the hit sitcom).

Standard comic situations are supplemented by wine snobbery and a mismatch, let us say, of gastronomic cultures, compensated by a romantic match) I hope the story opens up like a good wine.

21 June 2025

It is great that many projects at this Pilot are devoted precisely to women and their problems.

Feyk (“Fake”) is a wonderful illustration of complete defencelessness on the internet, and following on from that, of maximum vulnerability before people who do not believe you and judge you. A real horror in which the monsters hide not under the bed but in the comments.

A kind-hearted biology teacher (Anastasia Krasovskaya, who for the second festival in a row gets heavy scenes of violence) runs into brutal online stalking: everything looks as if she is taking part in erotic correspondence under her own name. Her identity has literally been stolen. The young woman tries to find the culprit, and this gives the proceedings a swift pace and the right nerve. It is frightening and intriguing how this story might end ⬅️

The History of Concrete: John Wilson Turns His Gaze Right Under His Feet

In short

“The History of Concrete” is a 2026 film directed by John Wilson. Towards the end the film transforms altogether into a philosophical reflection on the fragility of human nature in our thoroughly material world.

BIBetsy Isakova·4 June 2026

About the film

Title
The History of Concrete
Director
John Wilson
Year
2026

Starting today is the Beat Film Festival (the Moscow documentary festival), which has a most interesting programme this year (including on Kinopoisk, Russia’s largest film database and streaming service, so you can watch right at home).

I managed to see in advance the film The History of Concrete by John Wilson, the creator of the absurdist HBO show How To with John Wilson. The director continues to observe the contradictory everyday life of New York, now turning his gaze right under his feet, to concrete, in whose history we as humanity take insufficient interest.

He makes this choice in the midst of a creative crisis, after his collaboration with a major streaming service has come to an end (the inner workings of creativity get into the frame here too, which we always appreciate). Together with John we find ourselves at a screenwriting masterclass and learn the secrets of making “soap” that succeeds with the mass audience (oh God, I felt ill at how the ingredients overlapped with Heated Rivalry point by point, but I pretended not to see or hear). The director decides to follow the soap-makers’ instructions and to shoot his history of concrete by precisely these principles.

Among the documentary observations are shots from the distinctive corporate life of the concrete industry and rambling conversations with strangers that at times startle the ear (a couple of interlocutors told the director that “there isn’t too much concrete in cities, the problem is that there are too many people in cities”). Towards the end the film transforms altogether into a philosophical reflection on the fragility of human nature in our thoroughly material world. In short, you will definitely not be left without discoveries after watching: do not miss it if you like looking at the world from a new angle. And I will be back with other gems from the programme.
